Permitting becomes the new industrial policy for compute

Governments competing for AI infrastructure are discovering that approval speed outbids subsidy size.

Jonathan Bright · Policy Editor
August 2, 20262 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The jurisdictions winning data center investment have converged on an insight that reorders industrial policy: developers price time more than money. Fast-tracked permitting, pre-approved sites with secured interconnection, and single-window review are outcompeting larger subsidies attached to slower processes.

The policy machinery is adapting visibly. Regions are assembling shovel-ready compute campuses the way earlier eras assembled industrial parks, with environmental review, grid studies, and zoning resolved before a tenant appears. Officials describe the shift as selling certainty rather than writing checks.

RelatedStates are right to compete for the data center boom ›

The tension is democratic: acceleration compresses exactly the consultation that gives communities leverage over power, water, and land questions. The jurisdictions handling it durably are pairing speed with standing commitments, ratepayer protections and infrastructure contributions set in advance, so that fast does not have to mean unaccountable.
