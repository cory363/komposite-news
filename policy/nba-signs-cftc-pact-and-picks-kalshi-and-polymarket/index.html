NBA signs CFTC pact and picks Kalshi and Polymarket

The NBA on Monday became the third major US league to sign an information-sharing memorandum with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission, and named Kalshi and Polymarket its preferred prediction-market exchanges. The memorandum itself obliges neither side to share anything.

Jonathan Bright · House byline
October 26, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The National Basketball Association signed an information-sharing memorandum of understanding with the Commodity Futures Trading Commission on Monday, the agency said, and named Kalshi and Polymarket as its "Preferred Prediction Market Exchanges". The memorandum covers the integrity of professional basketball and the event contracts tied to it. The NBA is the third major US league to sign such a memorandum with the CFTC, after Major League Baseball and the National Hockey League. Event contracts are exchange-traded contracts that pay out according to the outcome of a future event, such as a game.

The memorandum is short and expressly non-binding. It calls itself "a statement of intent" and says it "does not require either Party to create or maintain information or to provide or share information with the other Party"; all sharing is "at the sole discretion of each Party", and it creates no "legally binding rights or obligations". The NBA may pass information from the CFTC to its Board of Governors but not to teams unless the agency agrees, which both sides expect it would for an active integrity investigation. Each side names a point of contact: Tyler Badgley, the CFTC's general counsel, and Dan Spillane, an NBA executive vice president and assistant general counsel. Either party can end it on 30 days' written notice. The copy the CFTC published leaves the execution date blank.

"The NBA joins the MLB and NHL in taking a responsible step toward protecting sports integrity and market participants from abuses in prediction markets like insider trading and fraud," said Michael Selig, the CFTC's chairman. Scott Kaufman-Ross, the NBA's head of media distribution and partnerships, said: "As this category develops, we believe it is important to work constructively with regulators and operators to help ensure that appropriate safeguards are in place."

The enforceable commitments sit in the league's commercial contracts, not in the memorandum. According to the NBA, its agreements with Kalshi and Polymarket include limits on which markets may be listed, prohibitions on trading NBA event contracts by league and team personnel, and obligations to share information about investigations and suspicious trading. In return the two exchanges get rights to use NBA intellectual property, marketing and promotion across US media, and official league data from Sportradar to run and settle markets. The league has also extended its agreement with DraftKings to cover prediction markets and says it will add other betting partners "in the coming weeks". The financial terms were not disclosed.

That is the distinction our reading of the two documents draws: the CFTC memorandum is a channel the regulator and the league may use, while the integrity rules are private terms between the league and the venues it has chosen to license. The CFTC's three league memorandums so far have arrived months apart.

Not every league has joined. The National Football League has rejected a similar arrangement and sided with states arguing that their gambling laws should govern sports contracts, CoinDesk reported. Komposite reported this month that the NFL asked the Supreme Court to hear New Jersey's case against Kalshi, describing the approach of the CFTC and the exchanges as "laissez-faire". Kalshi's response in that case is due on 9 November.

Why it matters: three federal appeals courts have split on whether sports event contracts fall under the CFTC or state gaming regulators, and the NBA has now paired a federal information channel with commercial deals with the exchanges that list its games. The CFTC has meanwhile proposed defining sports and other event contracts as swaps, which would put them more firmly inside its jurisdiction. A memorandum with no duty to share is a modest tool; the NBA's contracts with Kalshi and Polymarket are where its integrity terms will be tested.
