The Entity List has not grown since October 2025

Commerce has not added a single name to its main export blacklist for 401 days, by our count of the Federal Register. The Treasury Secretary said on Friday that four agencies wanted Megaspeed listed for months. Our count, and the rule Commerce extended this week.

Jonathan Bright · House byline
November 14, 20266 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The Commerce Department has not added a single company or person to the Entity List, Washington's main export blacklist, since 9 October 2025, by Komposite's count of rules in the Federal Register. That is 401 days. On Friday the Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessent, said four other departments had wanted one company, Megaspeed, on the list "for months".

"Treasury, War, USTR and State — they were in favor of putting Megaspeed on the entity list for months," Bessent said on CNBC's Squawk Box on Friday 13 November, according to the network's transcript. "But Commerce has the pin — Commerce BIS has the pin." The transcript's "pin" is the idiom Fortune and other outlets rendered as "pen": the Bureau of Industry and Security, part of Commerce, writes the list. "And you will have to ask them about their internal process," Bessent said.

Commerce has disputed a related account. Kristen Eichamer, a department spokeswoman, told Axios that Bessent and Howard Lutnick, the commerce secretary, had "no such conversation" about Megaspeed in the White House Situation Room, TNW reported, and an unnamed spokesperson told Semafor that "Treasury and Commerce have a great working relationship." Asked about the meeting on Friday, Bessent said: "We don't repeat conversations in the Situation Room." Megaspeed, a Singapore-based cloud company, has said inspections found no evidence of violations, as Komposite has reported, and Nvidia barred sales and shipments to it in June 2025, according to a spokesman quoted by The Wall Street Journal.

How long has the list been frozen?

About twice as long as any earlier gap since 2018. Komposite searched all rules from the Bureau of Industry and Security that mention the Entity List and counted those whose titles announce additions. In every year from 2018 to 2024 there were between seven and 17. In 2025 there were six, the last on Thursday 9 October 2025, adding 29 entries covering 26 entities in China, Turkey and the United Arab Emirates. In 2026 there have been none. The longest previous gap between such rules in our count was 196 days, from October 2018 to May 2019.

We also read every rule the bureau has published since 9 October 2025: 16, including a correction. None adds a name. Two take names off. A rule of 12 November 2025 removed one Chinese entity and six aliases of another, and a rule of 24 August 2026 removed one entity in Turkey, with a companion rule removing two Hong Kong addresses. The freeze is not a matter of one company; by our count no name of any kind has gone on the list in 13 months.

The dates line up awkwardly with the Megaspeed file. The last additions were published the day before Singapore's police confirmed, on 10 October 2025, that they were investigating Megaspeed, and four days before Senator Tom Cotton's letter of 13 October 2025 asked Lutnick to investigate it, as Komposite has reported. Nothing in the Federal Register since then mentions the company.

The last full year of additions was not small. The four rules published after the change of administration in January 2025 added 140 entities. One of them, the rule of 16 September 2025, included a single entity in Singapore among 32.

What does the trade truce have to do with it?

The pause began weeks before a deal with Beijing, and the deal froze a related rule. On 30 September 2025 the bureau published an interim rule under which any company at least 50 per cent owned by listed entities would automatically face the same restrictions, a standard it said was "designed to be consistent with longstanding Department of the Treasury practice". It was in force for 41 days. A White House fact sheet of 1 November 2025 said the United States would "suspend for one year, starting on November 10, 2025" that rule, as part of an agreement under which China would suspend rare-earth export controls "that it announced on October 9, 2025" — the same day as the last American additions.

The suspension rule said that "BIS will continue to evaluate U.S. national security and foreign policy interests related to these non-listed foreign affiliates of listed entities" and scheduled the 50 per cent rule to return on 10 November 2026. It did not return. A final rule published on Monday 9 November extends the suspension "through January 10, 2027", 62 days, and gives no reason. Its footer shows it was filed with the Federal Register at 4:15 p.m. on Thursday 5 November. The rule also misdates its subject: its background says the affiliates rule was published "On September 20, 2025", while the dates paragraph of the same rule, and the Federal Register itself, say 30 September.

Why would a listing matter for Megaspeed?

A listing turns a company into a licence requirement. The Export Control Reform Act, cited in this week's rule, authorises Commerce to maintain "a list of foreign persons and end uses that are determined to be a threat to the national security and foreign policy of the United States" and to restrict exports to them. Without a listing, suppliers rely on their own checks. Singapore's police have charged five people and six companies in a parallel server case, and the island's interior ministry said Washington had given "limited" help on Megaspeed, as Komposite reported in our timeline of that inquiry.

Megaspeed does not appear in American corporate disclosure at all. An SEC full-text search for the name across filings since January 2024 returns no results, by our check on Saturday, so no US-listed company has named it as a customer, supplier or risk. Bessent's broader complaint on Friday was about that gap. "I think there is a lot of leakage in the system and we are constantly working to close that down," he said, adding that chips had gone to "what were obviously pass-through shell companies".

Why it matters: the Entity List is the export system's most direct tool, and for 13 months it has not been used to add anyone, while the more automatic 50 per cent rule has been suspended as a bargaining chip with Beijing. Agencies that favour listing are now saying so in public, and the agency that decides has not explained its process. Earlier reporting set out how Commerce's own Megaspeed inquiry was paused at the Justice Department's request.

The next fixed dates are close. Nvidia reports results on Tuesday 17 November. Lutnick has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December, Fortune reported. The suspension of the 50 per cent rule runs out on Sunday 10 January 2027, 57 days from now, unless Commerce extends it again.
