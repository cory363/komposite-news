Digital identity credentials find enterprise traction

Verifiable credentials are being deployed for supplier onboarding and workforce verification, where the cost of manual checks is easiest to measure.

Sam Porter · Cybersecurity Reporter
August 7, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Digital identity is finding its market in the least glamorous places. Verifiable credentials, cryptographically signed attestations that can be checked without calling the issuer, are being deployed for supplier onboarding, contractor verification, and professional licensing, where enterprises can count exactly what manual checking costs today.

The design pattern that is working is narrow and bilateral. Rather than universal identity schemes, deployments involve a defined issuer, a defined verifier, and a specific claim: this company holds this certification, this worker completed this training. Each verified claim replaces a phone call, a PDF, or a notarized copy.

Blockchain's role is quieter than early advocates imagined. Ledgers anchor revocation registries and issuer keys, while the credentials themselves move over ordinary channels. Practitioners describe the architecture as blockchain-adjacent, using the shared ledger where a shared record is genuinely needed and nowhere else.

Adoption is proceeding sector by sector, led by industries with heavy compliance loads, logistics, construction, healthcare staffing. The pattern suggests identity infrastructure will arrive the way most infrastructure does: invisibly, and justified by a spreadsheet.
