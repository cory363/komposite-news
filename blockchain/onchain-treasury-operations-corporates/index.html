Corporate treasuries experiment with on-chain operations

A small but growing group of companies is holding working balances in tokenized cash equivalents, trading yield and settlement speed against new operational risk.

Elena Vasquez · Fintech Correspondent
July 30, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

A quiet experiment is running inside corporate treasury departments. Companies with heavy cross-border flows have begun holding a working slice of their cash in tokenized money market funds and regulated stablecoins, instruments that earn yield or settle instantly, and sometimes both.

The motivation is the gap between banking hours and business hours. A treasury that can move value on weekends and settle supplier payments in minutes carries lower buffers in every currency, and the released cash is measurable. Treasurers describe the allocation not as an investment thesis but as an operations upgrade.

The risks are equally operational. Key management, counterparty exposure to issuers, and accounting treatment all require new controls, and auditors are still standardizing their approach. Most programs cap on-chain balances at low single-digit percentages of cash while the control environment matures.

Service providers see the pattern and are building for it: custody with corporate-grade permissions, treasury dashboards that show on-chain and bank balances side by side, and policy engines that enforce who can move what. The infrastructure looks less like crypto and more like the treasury workstation, which is precisely the point.
