Stablecoin settlement volumes expand across B2B payments

Cross-border supplier payments are becoming the workhorse use case, with treasurers drawn to settlement speed rather than ideology.

Marcus Oyelaran · Blockchain & Digital Assets Editor
First published August 16, 2026 · Updated August 16, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The stablecoin story has moved from trading desks to accounts payable. Payment providers report sustained growth in business-to-business settlement volume, led by cross-border supplier payments in corridors where correspondent banking is slow, expensive, or both.

Treasurers describe the appeal in operational terms. A payment that settles in minutes with a final, auditable record compares favorably to a wire that takes days and returns errors by fax-era processes. Ideological arguments about money rarely feature; cutoff times and reconciliation costs do.

The infrastructure serving this demand looks increasingly like conventional fintech. Licensed issuers, regulated on-ramps, compliance screening, and treasury dashboards wrap the underlying rails so completely that some corporate users interact with a stablecoin without ever seeing one.

Growth is drawing scrutiny in proportion. Banking groups have pressed regulators on reserve standards and redemption rights, and policymakers in several jurisdictions are finalizing frameworks that will determine whether the current expansion consolidates around a few large issuers or fragments along regional lines.
