Chainalysis reports its own intruder

Chainalysis, the blockchain analytics firm tracing this week's Binance theft, said an attacker using "offensive agentic techniques" reached its internal tools and customers' names, emails and role labels. It found no sign that investigation data or credentials were taken.

Sam Porter · House byline
October 31, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Chainalysis, the blockchain analytics company, said an outside attacker using "offensive agentic techniques" gained access to its systems and internal tools and reached customers' names and email addresses, in a security notice dated Friday. It said it had found no evidence that investigation data or login credentials were accessed.

The data reached, according to the notice, was user IDs, names, email addresses and organisation role labels, which The Block described as belonging to provisioned users, the staff that client organisations set up on the platform. Chainalysis said it had found no evidence of access to investigation content such as graphs, notes and cases, to query results, or to clear-text passwords, multi-factor authentication secrets or session tokens, on the evidence "available to date". It is still working out which records belonged to which customer and said it would send each organisation its own update. The company said the intrusion was "not related to the recent crypto theft at Binance".

Chainalysis published the notice, together with the email it sent customers, at about 5:14pm Eastern time on Friday, BleepingComputer reported, and a spokesperson confirmed the incident to the publication. The breach was first flagged by International Cyber Digest, The Block reported. The company has brought in outside cybersecurity specialists, is using large language models in a wider review of its platform and is patching vulnerabilities. It advised customers to turn on two-factor authentication and to watch for unexpected password-reset requests or sign-in prompts, and warned of scammers impersonating the company, particularly those offering to recover funds or to recruit.

"Agentic" describes AI systems that plan and carry out multi-step tasks, such as scanning, logging in and moving between tools, with limited human direction. Chainalysis has not said how such techniques were used against it. BleepingComputer noted that it is unclear whether the attacker used an existing framework such as ARTEX or custom agents. Chainalysis declined to answer specific follow-up questions from The Block, which listed what the company had not disclosed: when it detected the intrusion, how many customers were affected, which vulnerabilities were exploited, the attacker's identity, and the evidence behind its statement that the incident was unrelated to Binance.

This is the third disclosure of an agent-assisted intrusion in ten days, by our count of Komposite's own reporting. On 21 October Microsoft said a China state-aligned group had used an open-source agent framework to try more than 2,250 customer tenants after breaking into ConnectWise's cloud backup service. On Thursday CrowdStrike said a group it calls Vapor Panda used the ARTEX framework against more than 200 organisations, nine of them confirmed compromised. In the first two, the disclosure came from the investigator; this time it comes from the company that was breached, and one whose business is investigation.

The timing is awkward. Chainalysis is one of the firms tracing the funds stolen from Binance, a theft it puts at $6.4bn. On Saturday it said the thieves had moved stablecoins through JustLend, a lending protocol on the Tron blockchain, and swapped them for ether, writing that "now the exploiter is free of worry of a freeze", The Block reported. Issuers such as Circle and Tether have frozen about $2.8bn of stablecoins, Chainalysis said.

Why it matters: the records taken are not secrets, but together they form a directory of who at Chainalysis's client organisations uses a crypto-tracing service, and in what role. That is the material targeted phishing is built from, which is why the company's own advice centres on password-reset prompts and impersonators. Binance's loss began with one engineer's laptop, as Komposite reported on Friday.
