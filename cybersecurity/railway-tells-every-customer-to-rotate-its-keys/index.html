Railway tells every customer to rotate its keys

Railway, a cloud platform for hosting applications, said an unauthorised party may have read and decrypted some customers' environment variables. It paused all deployments and told every user to roll their secrets, although it believes only a subset was affected.

Sam Porter · Cybersecurity Reporter
October 22, 20262 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Railway, a cloud platform for hosting applications, has told all users to rotate their API keys and credentials because an unauthorised party may have decrypted customer secrets, it said on Wednesday. It has paused all deployments while it investigates.

"Earlier this morning, Railway detected anomalous activity involving systems that store customer environment variables," the company wrote in the post, signed by Jacob Cooper. "Our investigation to date indicates that an unauthorized third party may have accessed encrypted environment variables and, for some of them, obtained the decrypted contents." Environment variables, it said, "may contain secrets such as API keys or database and registry credentials".

The scope is unclear. "While our initial analysis suggests the impact may be limited to a subset of users, we are nevertheless recommending that all users take the actions described below as a precaution," Railway said. It asked customers to "immediately roll all environment variables, including any API keys, stored in Railway, as well as any private registry credentials, and review logs for those services for unusual activity." It said it would email affected customers once it confirms which accounts were hit.

The pause is the operational cost. "All deployments are paused while we investigate. Until builds are restored, you will not be able to roll out new versions," the post said. TechCrunch reported that the pause effectively froze customers' websites and apps, and that anyone holding the stolen secrets could log in to linked services as the rightful owner. Railway later published separate guidance on checking for anomalous Google Cloud access and anomalous AWS access.

Railway has not said how many users were affected, how the intruder got in or which systems were compromised. It did not respond to TechCrunch's questions, including whether it had received a ransom demand, the outlet said. The company offered its own explanation: "We believe this is part of a series of attacks recently targeting platform companies with the aim of compromising AI inference providers. We have notified these upstream providers to revoke all keys proactively." That claim is Railway's and has not been independently verified.

There is a recent case of the kind Railway describes. OpenCode, an open-source AI coding tool, said on Saturday 17 October that attackers broke into an older system and reached stored provider keys belonging to 1.4 per cent of users of its OpenCode Console product, TechCrunch reported. OpenCode called it a "large-scale distillation attack", a label some observers disputed. Anthropic and Google blocked keys they believed were exposed, and OpenAI said it worked with OpenCode to refund API customers, according to TechCrunch. Nothing published links the two incidents beyond Railway's own description.

Why it matters: a platform that stores other companies' secrets turns one intrusion into many, and rotating keys is harder while deployments are frozen. For developers who keep model-provider keys in such platforms, the exposure is also a bill, since stolen keys can run up usage charges. Komposite reported this week on eight certificate authority keys and, earlier this month, on a support tool that held the keys.
