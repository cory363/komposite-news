Recipe of the Day

Short ribs that repay an afternoon

Three hours in a low oven, almost none of it yours. The work is a hard sear at the start and a skim at the end.

By Marta Reyes, Food Editor
3 hours 30 minutes · Serves 4 · Easy
Gennady Zakharin via Unsplash
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Why we’re making it

Short rib is the cut that makes a cook look better than they are. It is heavily marbled and full of connective tissue, which means it is unpleasant cooked quickly and forgiving cooked slowly. Left alone at a low temperature the collagen turns to gelatin, and the meat goes from chewy to something you serve with a spoon.

The only step that rewards attention is the sear. Everything after it is waiting. Do not rush the browning and do not crowd the pan, because the crust is where most of the finished flavour comes from and a pan packed with cold meat steams instead of browns.

Published September 6, 2026.

Ingredients

  • 4 lb bone-in beef short ribs, cut into individual ribs
  • Kosher salt and coarsely ground black pepper
  • 2 tbsp neutral oil
  • 2 onions, cut into thick wedges
  • 2 carrots, cut into large batons
  • 1 head garlic, halved crosswise
  • 2 tbsp tomato paste
  • 1 bottle dry red wine, nothing you would refuse to drink
  • 2 cups beef or chicken stock
  • 3 sprigs thyme and 2 bay leaves
  • Flaky salt and parsley, to finish

Method

  1. Heat the oven to 300°F. Season the ribs on every side, more heavily than feels reasonable, and leave them at room temperature while the oven comes up.
  2. Set a heavy lidded pot over medium-high with the oil. Brown the ribs in two or three batches, four to five minutes a side, until each one has a genuine dark crust. Crowding the pan is the one mistake that cannot be recovered later. Set them aside.
  3. Pour off all but a spoonful of fat. Add the onions, carrots and the garlic cut-side down and cook until they take colour, about eight minutes. Stir in the tomato paste and cook for another minute until it darkens.
  4. Add the wine and bring it to a boil, scraping the bottom until nothing is stuck. Let it reduce by about half, which takes ten minutes and is not optional: unreduced wine tastes sharp in the finished dish.
  5. Return the ribs and any juices, add the stock so the meat is about two-thirds submerged, tuck in the thyme and bay, cover, and put it in the oven for three hours.
  6. Check at three hours. A fork should turn in the meat with no resistance. If it resists, give it another thirty minutes; short rib does not overcook in a braise the way a lean cut does.
  7. Lift the ribs onto a platter. Skim the fat from the surface of the liquid, then reduce it on the stove for ten minutes until it coats a spoon. Taste and salt it properly.
  8. Spoon the sauce over the ribs, scatter parsley and flaky salt, and serve with something that will absorb the sauce.

If you are getting ahead

It is better the next day and it reheats without complaint. Cool it in the pot, refrigerate whole, and lift off the set fat before warming it through at a low heat. If anything, make it the day before on purpose.
