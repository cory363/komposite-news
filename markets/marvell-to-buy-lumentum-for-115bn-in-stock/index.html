A hundred and fifteen billion, paid in shares

Marvell agreed on Monday to buy Lumentum in an all-stock deal that values the AI laser maker at $115bn, or about $1,132 a share. The companies expect to close in the first half of 2028, after a date the contract treats as the first deadline.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
November 2, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Marvell Technology has agreed to buy Lumentum Holdings, which makes lasers and optical parts for AI data centres, in an all-stock deal that values Lumentum at $115bn, the two companies said on Monday. Lumentum shareholders will receive 4.500 Marvell shares for each share they hold, worth about $1,132 a share. That is a premium of about 23.9 per cent to both companies' closing prices on 28 October, which they describe as the last trading day before speculation about a deal began.

The merger agreement was signed on Sunday, 1 November, according to Marvell's filing with the Securities and Exchange Commission. Marvell will absorb Lumentum through two merger subsidiaries, and after closing its shareholders will own about 67 per cent of the combined company and Lumentum's about 33 per cent. Both boards approved the deal unanimously. It needs a vote of Lumentum's shareholders, and Marvell's shareholders must approve both the new shares and a change to Marvell's charter raising the number of shares it is allowed to issue. It also needs the expiry of the US antitrust waiting period under the Hart-Scott-Rodino Act "and the receipt of certain other required regulatory approvals", which the filing does not name.

The contract prices the risk of failure. If Marvell's board withdraws its recommendation or walks away for a better offer, Marvell pays Lumentum $5.8bn; the reciprocal fee for Lumentum is $2.9bn. If the deal is blocked or refused by regulators, or runs out of time, while only the regulatory conditions remain open, Marvell owes Lumentum $3.2bn. The first deadline in the agreement is 11:59pm Pacific time on 1 November 2027, which can be extended to 1 February, 1 May and 1 August 2028 if regulatory approvals are still outstanding. The companies expect to close in the first half of 2028. By our reading, that timetable assumes at least one of those extensions will be needed.

The executives framed the deal as a bet on light replacing copper. "AI infrastructure is undergoing a fundamental architectural shift toward optical connectivity, creating an opportunity that comes along once in a generation," said Matt Murphy, Marvell's chairman and chief executive, who will run the combined company. "Joining Marvell creates a company uniquely positioned to capitalize on a market opportunity of $550 billion and generate significant, sustainable stockholder value," said Michael Hurlston, Lumentum's president and chief executive. The $550bn market estimate is the companies' own. Hurlston and two other Lumentum directors will join Marvell's board, which now has seven members, taking it to ten by our count.

What is Marvell buying? On the same morning Lumentum reported revenue of $1,262.6m for the quarter to 26 September, up 136.5 per cent from a year earlier, with net income of $360.3m. Components such as lasers made up $754.2m and systems $508.4m. Hurlston said Lumentum had won a second major order for near-packaged or co-packaged optics, which put optical connections next to or inside the chip package, "backed by purchase commitments exceeding $5 billion". Lumentum forecast $1.575bn to $1.625bn of revenue for the December quarter; by our arithmetic the midpoint is $337m above the quarter just reported.

By our arithmetic, $115bn is 22.8 times Lumentum's annualised September-quarter revenue, and 18.0 times the annualised midpoint of its guidance. The companies' figures also imply a Marvell share price of about $251.6 and an unaffected Lumentum price of about $914, though both are derived from rounded numbers. In return, the companies promise more than $1bn a year of cost savings at full run rate and say the deal will add to Marvell's adjusted earnings per share in its first year after closing. Those are forecasts. Marvell said its revenue for the quarter now under way would be consistent with its earlier guidance, narrowing the range to plus or minus 2 per cent from 5 per cent, without giving a figure.

Lumentum's pitch to buyers has been scarcity. Its chief executive said last month that its AI optical parts were sold out to early 2029, a year later than he had said in the spring. Because the price is paid in shares, no cash changes hands for Lumentum's stock, at a time when other parts of the AI supply chain are leaning on borrowed money, as Nvidia's talks to guarantee Nscale's financing showed on Sunday.

Why it matters: an AI data centre is increasingly limited by how fast chips can talk to each other, and the companies argue that copper is reaching its physical limits. Combining Marvell's chip design with Lumentum's lasers and its indium phosphide manufacturing would put more of that link under one roof. That is also what antitrust reviewers will look at, and the contract gives them until August 2028 before Marvell's $3.2bn fee comes into play.
