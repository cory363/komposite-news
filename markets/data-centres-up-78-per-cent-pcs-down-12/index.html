Data centres up 78 per cent, PCs down 12

Intel's server chip sales rose 78 per cent in the third quarter and it beat profit forecasts, but it guided fourth-quarter revenue and margins lower, citing a PC market it expects to shrink about 12 per cent this year. The shares fell by about a fifth after hours.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 30, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Intel's data centre and AI revenue rose 78 per cent to $7.3bn in the third quarter, but the company forecast lower fourth-quarter revenue and margins on Thursday because of a shrinking PC market, according to its results release. The shares fell about 21 per cent in after-hours trading.

Total revenue was $16.5bn, up 21 per cent from $13.7bn a year earlier, and non-GAAP earnings were 52 cents a share against 23 cents. The non-GAAP gross margin rose to 44.1 per cent from 40.0 per cent. Intel Foundry, the manufacturing arm, reported $6.7bn, up 58 per cent. Client computing, the PC business, fell 5 per cent to $8.1bn. For the fourth quarter Intel guided revenue of $15.0bn to $16.0bn, a non-GAAP gross margin of 38.0 per cent and earnings of 25 cents a share.

"Despite near-term headwinds across the PC industry, demand for our CPUs remains robust," Lip-Bu Tan, the chief executive, said in the release, adding that the "immediate priority is to unlock additional supply to meet extraordinary demand for our Xeon family". David Zinsner, the chief financial officer, said: "we see the PC slowdown continuing at least through the first half of next year."

On the call, Zinsner put a number on the slowdown. "We expect the 2026 PC unit TAM to decline approximately 12%, with second-half units down more than 20%," he said, according to the transcript. Tan blamed "the unprecedented memory crunch", as prices and shortages of memory chips push up the cost of PCs. Zinsner also said Intel's Fab 28 in Israel had been shut for about two weeks, adding: "I think the current thinking is that we will have about a $300 million impact to the fourth quarter." He put the cost at about two points of gross margin.

The market weighed the guidance over the beat. Analysts had expected 40 cents a share on revenue of $16.48bn, Investing.com reported, so Intel beat on profit by 12 cents. The stock closed the regular session at $92.07 and traded at $72.85 after hours, down $19.22. On Friday morning the fall in premarket trading was more than 25 per cent, Fox News reported, citing Tan's remark that "the pace of this PC correction has accelerated over the last two months".

By our arithmetic, the guidance midpoint of $15.5bn is 6 per cent below the third quarter, and the margin guide is 6.1 points lower, of which the Israel plant explains about 2. The rest comes from the PC mix and supply costs Intel describes. Cash was thinner than the headline profit: operating cash flow of $5.1bn less $4.2bn of gross capital spending leaves $0.9bn, against the $1.6bn of adjusted free cash flow Intel reports after its own adjustments.

Why it matters: the memory shortage that is lifting chipmakers such as Samsung, which forecast a record quarterly profit on AI memory demand this month, is a cost for companies that sell into PCs. Intel's server business is growing as fast as it can be supplied, but the PC business is still the larger of the two. The fall came a day after Alphabet's class A shares dropped 12.64 per cent, about $500bn of market value, Fox News reported, following results that showed capital spending running ahead of operating cash.
