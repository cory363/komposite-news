Eighty-five per cent margins, and an eight-for-one split

Sandisk's revenue rose to $11.0bn in its fiscal first quarter, nearly five times a year earlier, at an 85 per cent gross margin. It guided higher again, bought back $5bn of stock and will split its shares eight for one from 20 November.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 30, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Sandisk, the flash memory maker, reported revenue of $11.0bn for its fiscal first quarter on Thursday, up 378 per cent from a year earlier, at a gross margin of 85 per cent, according to its results release. It also announced an eight-for-one stock split.

Revenue for the quarter to 2 October was $11,031m, against $8,965m in the previous quarter and $2,308m a year earlier. GAAP operating income was $8,780m, up from $176m, and GAAP net income was $8,101m, or $52.60 a diluted share; on Sandisk's non-GAAP measure, earnings were $49.51 a share. The GAAP gross margin was 85.0 per cent, against 29.8 per cent a year before. Operating cash flow was $6,363m and free cash flow $6,309m. For the second quarter Sandisk guided revenue of $12.2bn to $12.6bn, a non-GAAP gross margin of 84.5 to 85.5 per cent and non-GAAP earnings of $55 to $57 a share before the split.

"We have started fiscal year 2027 with another quarter of strong performance, with robust and growing demand for our products driving outpaced growth in our data center business," David Goeckeler, the chairman and chief executive, said in the release. Datacenter revenue rose 61 per cent from the previous quarter to $4,802m. Edge, which covers flash sold into phones, PCs and other devices, rose 7 per cent to $5,832m. Consumer products fell 29 per cent to $397m.

On the call, Goeckeler said customers "have underestimated the amount of NAND they need", and that "we have multiple customers coming back for a second round", 24/7 Wall St. reported. Sandisk calls its long-term supply contracts, which include customer prepayments, its "new business model". It had signed eight by its August investor day, covering about half of fiscal 2027 output and about two-thirds of fiscal 2028, and closed two more in the quarter, the report said. Management told analysts: "Our NBM pipeline exceeds our supply throughout the end of the decade." Luis Visoso, the chief financial officer, said pricing was expected to rise only "modestly".

Analysts had expected revenue of $10.7bn, according to S&P Global Market Intelligence figures cited by Yahoo Finance, and the midpoints of both revenue and earnings guidance were above Street forecasts of $12.23bn and $53.30 a share. The shares had closed Thursday's regular session at $1,330.05, down 5.3 per cent, before the release. Yahoo Finance noted that the stock was up more than 460 per cent this year but had "largely treaded water since July".

The split is mechanical but not trivial. Holders of record at the close on Monday 9 November receive seven additional shares for each share held after the close on Thursday 19 November, and the stock trades on a split-adjusted basis from Friday 20 November. By our arithmetic, Thursday's close becomes about $166 a share, and the guidance midpoint of $56 becomes about $7.00 a share on the new count. The split comes after a quarter in which Sandisk spent $5.0bn repurchasing about 3.2m shares, 79 per cent of its free cash flow by our arithmetic, and 6.0m shares, about 4 per cent of its stock, over two quarters.

By our arithmetic, the datacenter business has gone from 11.7 per cent of revenue to 43.5 per cent in a year, and is 18 times its size a year ago. GAAP operating income was 79.6 per cent of revenue. Guidance implies another 12 per cent of sequential growth at the midpoint, with no margin give-back. The guidance is the company's own, and its support is contractual rather than cyclical: the long-term agreements already cover about half of this fiscal year's output and about two-thirds of next year's, on 24/7 Wall St.'s account of the call.

Why it matters: the memory shortage has two sides. For the makers of memory it is the best market in their history, as Samsung's record quarterly profit forecast showed this month. For those who buy it, it is a cost: Intel on Thursday blamed "the unprecedented memory crunch" for a shrinking PC market, and its shares fell by about a fifth after hours, as Komposite reported on Friday. Sandisk's own consumer revenue fell 39 per cent from a year earlier.
