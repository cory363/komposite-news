A Microsoft redesign that earns IREN no delay credits

IREN's quarterly filing says Microsoft asked for a network design change across its four Texas deployments, and that the contract extends delivery dates without delay credits when Microsoft causes the delay. Its $4bn revenue run-rate target has slipped a month or two.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
November 9, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

"At Microsoft's request, we are implementing a network design change across the Horizon deployments," IREN told investors in its quarterly report filed on Thursday. The next sentence sets the terms. Under its contract with Microsoft, "delivery dates are extended, without delay credits, for delays resulting from a Microsoft-requested change". The Nasdaq-listed company, which turned from bitcoin mining to renting out AI computing, has four 50-megawatt Horizon deployments for Microsoft at Childress, Texas.

A delay credit is a payment or discount a supplier owes when it misses a delivery date. The clause means Microsoft cannot claim one for the delay it asked for. It does not say who pays for the redesign itself. The filing says the change "is being implemented in parallel with discussions regarding delivery dates and the application and payment of fees, credits and cost reimbursements". Those discussions are not finished, and IREN does not say what it has asked for.

What has slipped?

The date for IREN's headline target. In its August results, IREN said more than $4bn of annualised run-rate revenue, or ARR, was "targeted to be operational by December 31, 2026". ARR is the company's own measure: contracted hourly GPU prices for machines in service, multiplied by the 8,760 hours in a year. Thursday's release moves the target to 31 January 2027 "following a customer-requested change and supplier timing", adding that it "may extend to February 2027". By our count that is a slip of 31 to 59 days. The 10-Q's risk factors give a third cause, saying the capacity is now expected the following quarter "as a result of a customer-requested design change, supply timing and third-party delays".

The descriptions of the Horizons have also moved. In August, Horizon 1 had been delivered, Horizon 2 was in commissioning and Horizons 3 and 4 were in late-stage construction for delivery in the fourth quarter. In November, Horizon 1 is in service and Horizons 2 to 4 are all described as in late-stage construction. Daniel Roberts, IREN's co-founder and co-chief executive, said in the release that the contracted ARR "is funded and now targeted for January, after a customer-requested design change and supplier timing moved it from year-end".

What does waiting cost IREN?

The filing points to at least one running cost: interest on money it cannot yet spend. A financing subsidiary arranged $3,645m for the GPUs and infrastructure at Horizons 1 to 4: a $1,545m delayed-draw term loan and $2,100m of senior secured notes sold in a US private placement at 5.96 per cent. Note proceeds sit in escrow until release conditions are met. At 30 September, $1,050m was held there, and the notes "bear interest from their respective issue dates, including while the proceeds remain in escrow". One $525m tranche was released on 1 October. By our subtraction, that leaves $525m in escrow on the filing's account. Repayments on the first tranche began in September, after Microsoft accepted Horizon 1.

The quarter itself was IREN's largest yet. Revenue rose to $228m from $137m in the June quarter, and AI Cloud revenue reached $200m, of which $65m was lease revenue that the 10-Q says includes the Microsoft contract. Bitcoin mining brought in $28m, from 402 coins mined against 2,039 a year earlier, a fall of 80 per cent by our arithmetic. The net loss was $263m. The company put its revenue backlog at $20.3bn, up 22 per cent, with about two-thirds owed by investment-grade counterparties.

Is the rest of the build-out paid for?

Not all of it. IREN says the programme behind the $4bn target is "fully funded", a term its release defines to include undrawn facilities and prepayments not yet received, subject to conditions. Its wider plan is larger. Of $15bn of capital spending it still plans for the year to June 2027, $9bn is covered by cash and committed financing and $6bn is "target" financing, subject to market conditions: 40 per cent of the remainder, by our arithmetic. Contracted capital commitments rose 37 per cent in the quarter, to $18.95bn from $13.81bn. The August figure of $14bn in cash and committed funding, less the $5.1bn the company spent on investments during the quarter, comes to about the $9bn it now reports, so the two releases agree.

Why it matters: the AI infrastructure trade rests on delivery dates. Investors in neoclouds, the specialist GPU landlords, have been pricing in more risk, as Komposite reported when CoreWeave's bond yields neared 15 per cent, and much of the sector's debt is tied to contracts with a few large tenants. IREN's filing shows how much leverage a tenant like Microsoft has inside such a contract: it can change the design, and the supplier absorbs the wait. Who pays for the redesign is still being negotiated.

The next markers are delivery dates. IREN expects the Horizons to be handed over in the coming quarter, and its January target date for the ARR is 83 days away. The company owns the land and power under its sites, a legacy of its bitcoin-mining years, and says that gives it more ways to fund growth.
