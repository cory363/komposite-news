Nvidia's first report without Huang has a $108bn bar

Nvidia reports on Tuesday against its own forecast of $108bn of revenue, the first results since Jensen Huang resigned. It has beaten the midpoint of its forecast by between 2.5 and 5.7 per cent for six quarters. Our table, and what the call cannot settle.

Komposite News Staff
November 14, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Nvidia will report results for its third fiscal quarter on Tuesday 17 November against a revenue forecast of $108.0bn, plus or minus 2 per cent, which it set in August. It is the first report since Jensen Huang resigned as chief executive on 11 November. If the company beats its own midpoint by the same margins as in the past six quarters, revenue would land between about $110.7bn and $114.2bn, by our arithmetic.

The timetable is unchanged. Nvidia said in its call notice that results would be published at about 1:20 p.m. Pacific time, with a call at 2 p.m. limited to questions from financial analysts and institutional investors, and that written commentary would come from Colette Kress, the chief financial officer. Kress is now also interim chief executive, under the transition the company announced on Thursday, so the commentary and the call will both be hers.

How large have the beats been?

Larger in dollars every quarter, and steady as a share. Measured against the midpoint of each forecast, Nvidia's beat rose from $1.06bn in the quarter to April 2025 to $5.22bn in the quarter to July 2026. As a percentage it ranged from 2.5 to 5.7, with a mean of 4.5, by our arithmetic. Revenue came in above the top of the plus-or-minus 2 per cent range in each of the six quarters, though only by $202m in the first of them. This quarter the range runs from $105.84bn to $110.16bn; a figure below the bottom would be the first miss of the range in the table.

The forecasts themselves have grown more cautious relative to the last reported quarter. The $108bn figure is 12.2 per cent above the $96.2bn reported for the July quarter. The four forecasts before it implied growth of 15.5, 14.0, 14.5 and 11.5 per cent on the prior quarter, while actual sequential growth over the same stretch ran at 22.0, 19.5, 19.8 and 17.9 per cent. The exception in the table is the quarter to July 2025, when the forecast implied 2.1 per cent growth because export limits on the H20 chip took out about $8bn of expected sales.

China is outside the number. The August release said: "NVIDIA is not assuming any Data Center compute revenue from China in its outlook." That has been the company's language since February, as Komposite reported when the guidance first assumed China would buy nothing. Nvidia also guided gross margins of 74.0 per cent, plus or minus half a point, against 75.0 per cent in the July quarter, and operating expenses of about $9.2bn under US accounting rules.

What the release cannot settle

The results are the first formal occasion on which Nvidia's management can be asked about the events of the past fortnight, though the company decides which questions to take. Its 12 November release and the accompanying 8-K gave no reason for Huang's departure, as Komposite reported on Thursday. The board's independent inquiry into reports that Huang was targeted by a foreign intelligence effort has no published timetable. The share price has already absorbed a large part of the news: Nvidia lost $428bn of market value in two days earlier this month.

Washington adds its own calendar. Three House committee chairmen asked on 5 November for a closed briefing on 18 November, the day after the results; their letter was addressed to Huang, who no longer runs the company. Howard Lutnick, the commerce secretary, has agreed to testify before the Senate Banking Committee on 10 December, Fortune reported. On Friday the Treasury Secretary, Scott Bessent, said on CNBC that the question was whether Nvidia knew its clients, and that "anyone at the company there be held accountable" for chips that leaked to China, a theme we examine in our report on the Entity List.

Why it matters: Nvidia's forecast is the closest thing the AI build-out has to a monthly order book, and the quarterly beat has been part of how investors price it. A first set of numbers under an interim chief executive who is also the finance chief tests whether that pattern holds through a governance crisis. Data-centre revenue was $89.0bn in the July quarter, 92.5 per cent of the total, by our division, so the beat or miss will be almost entirely a data-centre number.

The forecast for the quarter to January 2027, due on Tuesday afternoon, will be the first Nvidia has set without Huang. Analysts will compare it with the $108bn of this quarter, and with the 12 per cent sequential step the company has asked them to expect.
