Equity Raise

Thirty-seven and a half billion, with sixty to follow

Meta priced $37.5bn of new equity on Monday, $25bn of common stock at $625 a share and $12.5bn of mandatory convertible preferred, and said it plans a further $60bn share-sale programme next year. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom indicated interest in buying up to $5bn.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 28, 20265 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Meta Platforms priced $37.5bn of new equity late on Monday, selling $25bn of Class A common stock at $625 a share and $12.5bn of mandatory convertible preferred stock, the company said in its pricing announcement. It also said it plans to sell about $60bn more stock through an at-the-market programme from next year.

The common stock sale was upsized to $25.0bn from $22.5bn and is structured as a forward sale. Affiliates of Morgan Stanley and Goldman Sachs, the two lead bookrunners, will borrow and sell 40m Class A shares now; Meta will deliver new shares and collect about $24.9bn, at an initial forward price of about $622.92, on dates of its choosing on or before 19 November 2027. Meta expects no proceeds from that initial sale of borrowed shares, the release said. The preferred stock is sold as 250m depositary shares at $50, each a twentieth of a share carrying a 6.50 per cent annual dividend, and converts into Class A stock around 15 August 2029. Both offerings are expected to close on Thursday, and the proceeds are for "general corporate purposes".

The third piece is not yet a deal. In a free writing prospectus filed with the SEC, Meta said it "plans to launch an approximately $60 billion at-the-market ('ATM') program" once its new non-voting Class C shares begin trading, and that the programme "is not expected to commence until the first quarter of 2027". It added that it "has not entered into any agreement" and that "the timing, size and terms of any such program, if established, have not been determined." An at-the-market programme lets a company sell shares into the market in small amounts over time rather than in one underwritten block.

Three chipmakers are on the buy side. The prospectus supplement names NVIDIA, AMD and Broadcom as "Cornerstone Investors" that "indicated an interest in purchasing up to an aggregate of $5.0 billion" of the common stock, about a fifth of the offering by our arithmetic, and says the indications are "not binding agreements or commitments to purchase". The Motley Fool reported that Nvidia said it would invest $3bn; Komposite has not seen that figure in a filing. The price of $625 was about 0.9 per cent below the last sale of $630.56 on Monday, by our arithmetic from that report, and the shares closed at $612.49 on Wednesday, down 1.24 per cent.

The reason is in the cash flow statement. Meta's third-quarter results, published on 21 October, show capital expenditure, including finance-lease principal, of $34.91bn in the quarter and free cash flow of minus $2.12bn. The company kept its 2026 capital spending forecast at $135bn to $145bn and made no share buybacks in the quarter. It ended September with $82.45bn of cash and marketable securities, and the preliminary prospectus puts total consolidated indebtedness at $83.7bn. Investing.com described the negative free cash flow as the first in more than a decade.

The dilution is modest on its own. By our arithmetic, the 40m new common shares equal 1.6 per cent of the 2,556m Class A and Class B shares outstanding on 30 September, and the preferred will convert into between 17.0m and 20.0m more, so the two priced deals add 2.2 to 2.3 per cent before over-allotment options, or up to 2.7 per cent if both are exercised in full. The full $60bn programme, at Monday's price, would be roughly another 3.8 per cent. At $625 a share, the $37.5bn priced is about 2.3 per cent of Meta's market value of roughly $1.6tn. The preferred dividend costs about $812.5m a year.

The order of the deals matters for control. Meta created the Class C shares, which carry no votes, so that it could raise equity without diluting Mark Zuckerberg, who held about 60.6 per cent of the voting power, as Komposite reported last week. Monday's sale, however, is of voting Class A shares, because the Class C stock does not take effect until 19 January. By our arithmetic from the prospectus share counts, 40m new Class A votes take Mr Zuckerberg's share to about 60.2 per cent, and conversion of the preferred into Class A would take it to about 59.9 per cent. The filings allow the forward sales and the preferred to be settled in Class C stock in some circumstances, which would avoid that.

Why it matters: Meta has paid for its data centres from operating cash and, increasingly, from bonds; The Motley Fool counts $29.9bn of long-term borrowing in the fourth quarter of 2025 and $24.9bn in the second quarter of 2026. It is now adding equity, with chipmakers among the would-be buyers. Against the midpoint of its 2026 spending forecast, the $37.5bn priced this week would cover about 27 per cent of a single year's capital expenditure, by our arithmetic, and the cash from the common stock is not due until as late as November 2027.

Other capital-intensive AI builders have reached for different structures. SpaceX took $40bn of GPU financing as a lease from an Apollo-managed vehicle, Komposite reported on Monday, and Apollo's chief economist argued at the weekend that the financial payoff from AI has so far stopped at seven companies, Meta among them. Meta has now asked public shareholders to fund more of the bet directly.
