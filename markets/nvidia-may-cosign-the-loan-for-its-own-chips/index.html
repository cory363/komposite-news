Nvidia may cosign the loan for its own chips

Nvidia is in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of debt for Nscale's West Virginia campus, where Anthropic has signed for up to $44.6bn of computing. Nscale's IPO filing says none of the financing for that contract was committed.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
November 1, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Nvidia is in talks to guarantee billions of dollars of debt for Nscale's AI data centre campus in West Virginia, The Information reported on Thursday, in an account Bloomberg corroborated, according to TechCrunch. No figure for the guarantee has been reported, and neither company has commented in the accounts we read.

The arrangement under discussion is a residual value guarantee. If Nscale defaulted on its construction payments, Nvidia would step in, temporarily take over the facility and help find a buyer and a new tenant, TechCrunch said. The money would pay for electricity and construction at the site, where the first building is planned at 460 megawatts and is expected to run Nvidia's Vera Rubin chips. TechCrunch noted that Nvidia's earlier support for cloud providers has mostly covered leases and chip purchases rather than construction.

The customer is Anthropic. On 25 August Nscale signed four agreements with the AI lab that provide for payments of up to about $44.6bn, subject to delivery and availability requirements, at its Monarch Compute Campus in Mason County, according to Nscale's registration statement. The filing describes Monarch as a site of about 2,250 acres with up to 8 gigawatts of gross power, bought in March for $792.6m. It also says Nscale must use its best efforts to obtain financing within a set period, and that "we have not obtained binding commitments for any of the financings required" to perform the contract. That was the position on 18 September, the date of the filing.

The scale of the gap is plain from the same document. Nscale reported revenue of $140.6m for the first half of 2026 and cash of $1.5bn at the end of June. By our arithmetic, the Anthropic contract is worth about 317 times the company's half-year revenue and about 30 times its cash. Nscale and Anthropic are also discussing a second deal of roughly $45bn for another building, TechCrunch reported. Nscale is seeking a valuation of up to $35bn in its listing.

Nvidia would not be new to guaranteeing Nscale's obligations. The filing shows that in October 2025 it agreed to guarantee up to $860.3m of Nscale's payments under a lease in Ward County, Texas, receiving warrants in return, with Nscale keeping $470.0m in escrow as Nvidia's recourse. Nvidia is also putting $1.0bn into Nscale's convertible financing. The Information puts Nvidia's investments and credit support for neoclouds, the specialist AI cloud providers, and other large customers at roughly $170bn, TechCrunch reported.

What would a guarantee change? A lender to a data centre builder is exposed to two risks: that the tenant walks away, and that the half-built asset is worth little to anyone else. A residual value guarantee from the chip supplier addresses the second, because Nvidia would stand behind the asset's value and help re-let it. It also ties more of Nvidia's balance sheet to demand for its own products, the point TechCrunch made when it described the chipmaker as offering to cosign the loan.

The talks come as investors mark down the businesses that rent out AI computing. Shares of CoreWeave, Nebius and IREN each fell more than 30 per cent in October, TechCrunch said, citing CNBC, and CoreWeave's 2032 bonds yielded 14.8 per cent earlier in the month, as Komposite reported. Anthropic, the tenant, made its own listing documents public last week, showing first-half revenue of $16.3bn and a net loss of $80.7bn.

Why it matters: the AI build-out is being financed further and further from the companies that will use it, and the risk is being passed back to the supplier. Komposite reported in September that Nscale's backlog has two names on it, Microsoft and Anthropic, and that the Anthropic contract was not yet financed. If Nvidia signs, the third name standing behind that campus will be the company selling the chips.
