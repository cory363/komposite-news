Jio lists a fifth higher, on three per cent of its shares

Jio Platforms opened at ₹1,358 on Wednesday, 21 per cent above its ₹1,119 offer price, after India's largest initial public offering. It closed at ₹1,419, valuing the Reliance telecoms unit at about ₹13.05 lakh crore.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 28, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Jio Platforms, Reliance Industries' telecoms and digital arm, opened at ₹1,358 on its debut on Wednesday, 21.36 per cent above its ₹1,119 offer price, after raising ₹30,213 crore in India's largest initial public offering, according to Upstox's listing-day coverage.

The shares, which trade on the National Stock Exchange (NSE) under the symbol JPL and on BSE, moved between ₹1,357 and ₹1,374.50 in the morning before rising in the afternoon and settling at ₹1,419 on the NSE, up 4.49 per cent on the opening price and, by our arithmetic, 26.8 per cent above the offer price. Upstox put the closing market value at ₹13,05,380.12 crore, or about ₹13.05 lakh crore (₹13.05tn). TechCrunch reported that the opening price valued Jio at about $131.9bn, making it India's second-most valuable listed company after its parent.

The offering was entirely new shares, 27 crore of them, priced at the top of a ₹1,065 to ₹1,119 range, with no existing investor selling. It opened on Wednesday 21 October and closed on Friday 23 October, Upstox reported, and was subscribed 22.19 times. TechCrunch put investor bids at more than $77bn from more than 13.8 million applicants. The issue surpassed Hyundai Motor India's 2024 offering as the country's largest, according to Upstox and Business Today. Anchor investors, Upstox reported, included LIC, BlackRock, Fidelity, J.P. Morgan, Capital Group and the Government of Singapore.

Most of the money is earmarked for debt. Up to ₹275bn, about 91 per cent of the proceeds by our arithmetic, will repay borrowings at the subsidiary Reliance Jio Infocomm, TechCrunch reported, against total borrowings of ₹613.7bn at the end of June. If all of it is used that way, that would retire about 45 per cent of the borrowings, by our arithmetic.

Akash Ambani, Jio's managing director, told employees the listing "is not our destination" but "a new beginning", according to Upstox. TechCrunch quoted him saying: "My dream is that one day, when the world counts its great technology companies, one of them will be from India." He said the platforms serve more than 540 million customers.

The float is the detail the headline numbers leave out. Dividing the closing market value by the closing price gives about 920 crore shares in issue, by our arithmetic; the 27 crore new shares are about 2.9 per cent of that. An offering that drew more than $77bn of bids was, in other words, a sale of less than a thirtieth of the company, and the first-day price was set by trading in that sliver.

Why it matters: Jio is now a listed comparator for telecoms and digital-services valuations in India, and its first-day price is a test of what the domestic market will pay for the Reliance group's technology businesses separately from the parent. At about 43 times last year's profit, on TechCrunch's figures, buyers are paying for growth rather than current earnings; revenue and profit each rose about 15 per cent in the year to March, TechCrunch reported. Jio reports second-quarter results on Friday, according to Upstox.

The debut contrasts with the reception for other large listings this autumn. In London, the biggest listing in five years opened below its price, and Komposite has reported that the reopened IPO window favours a few very large deals while most of the pipeline waits.
