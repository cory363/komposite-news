A third off the round, a tenth off the price

OpenAI has cut its funding target to about $20bn from $30bn and is now seeking a $1.25tn valuation, down from $1.4tn, the Financial Times reported. The Nasdaq fell 2.9 per cent on Friday, its worst day since June, and 5.3 per cent in October.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 31, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

OpenAI has cut the target for its latest funding round by a third, to about $20bn, and is seeking a valuation of $1.25tn rather than $1.4tn, the Financial Times reported on Friday, according to Reuters. The FT cited people with knowledge of the matter.

The first close of the round is expected to raise less than $20bn because some investors have reduced their allocations, and the deadline for commitments was Friday, Reuters said, relaying the FT. A consortium of investors from the United Arab Emirates that includes MGX, the state-backed Abu Dhabi investment firm, is expected to be the largest participant, and in return OpenAI would get an option to lease newly built computing capacity in the region. Founders Fund, Coatue, Sequoia Capital and Andreessen Horowitz are also expected to take part, and wealthy individuals may be able to invest later through a retail offering at the same $1.25tn valuation.

The cut in valuation came first. The $1.4tn figure was the original pitch for the round and was lowered to $1.25tn the previous week, TNW reported, adding that the Wall Street Journal had reported OpenAI raising less than half of its $30bn goal. The Decoder, citing the Journal, put commitments at about $11bn. That figure comes from a single report we could not read in the original, and OpenAI has not published any of these numbers. No comment from the company appears in the accounts we read.

What does the change amount to? By our arithmetic, the target is down by a third and the price by a tenth: $150bn off $1.4tn is 10.7 per cent. The new money would buy about 1.6 per cent of the company, against about 2.1 per cent under the original plan, as a simple ratio of the amount raised to the valuation. At $1.25tn, OpenAI would still be valued 46.7 per cent above the $852bn of its March round.

The public market moved the other way first. The Nasdaq Composite fell 747.73 points, or 2.86 per cent, to 25,437.00 on Friday, its largest one-day percentage drop since 5 June, after weak results from Intel and Amazon, Reuters reported. Intel's shares fell about 28.6 per cent. The S&P 500 lost 1.82 per cent to 7,580.07 and the Dow Jones Industrial Average 1.09 per cent to 51,770.31. The S&P 500 and the Nasdaq ended October lower, while the Dow finished the month up.

Set side by side, the two prices have not moved by the same amount. Since Reuters first reported the $30bn round, the Nasdaq Composite has fallen 3.8 per cent by our arithmetic, while the valuation OpenAI is seeking has come down 10.7 per cent. The private price is not set by trading, so the comparison is loose, but it is the clearest measure available of how far demand for the largest private AI company has cooled against the listed market.

OpenAI is pursuing a stock market listing in 2027, and it wants this round closed before Anthropic lists, Reuters said, because the two companies draw on many of the same investors. Anthropic made its registration statement public on Monday, showing first-half revenue of $16.3bn and a net loss of $80.7bn. Its borrowing outlook has also shifted. Blackstone, the anchor investor in a recent $63bn deal financing Anthropic's chips, has told investors it expects Anthropic to receive a "high" junk rating, double-B, after its listing, having earlier predicted an investment-grade rating before 2027, the FT reported, according to TechCrunch. TechCrunch noted that "Blackstone's forecast is just a prediction"; Anthropic and Blackstone declined to comment.

Why it matters: the two largest private AI labs are raising equity and debt in the same weeks that public investors are marking down the companies that supply and host them. Amazon spent $77.3bn on property and equipment in its third quarter, 182 per cent of its operating cash flow, and Alphabet raised $41.1bn of debt and stock to cover its own build-out. A smaller round at a lower price does not change what OpenAI must spend; it changes how much of the company it gives up to pay for it, as Komposite noted when the round was first reported at $30bn.
