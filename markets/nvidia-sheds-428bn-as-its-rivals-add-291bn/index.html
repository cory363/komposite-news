Nvidia sheds $428bn as its rivals add $291bn

Nvidia's shares fell 9.5 per cent on Monday and Tuesday after Jensen Huang admitted an affair, erasing about $428bn of value by our arithmetic. AMD and Broadcom added $291bn. A shareholder suit now claims a 396-day class period.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
November 11, 20266 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Nvidia's shares closed at $168.89 on Tuesday 10 November, 9.5 per cent below the $186.64 close of Friday 6 November, after two days of selling that followed chief executive Jensen Huang's admission of an affair. On the 24.1 billion shares in its latest quarterly filing, that is about $428bn of market value lost since Friday, by our arithmetic.

The fall came in two steps. On Monday the stock lost $7.97, or 4.27 per cent, about $192bn of value. On Tuesday it lost a further 5.47 per cent, CNBC reported, or about $236bn, by our multiplication. Nvidia briefly traded below a $4tn valuation on Tuesday afternoon before closing above it. On our share count the line sits at about $166 a share; the close left the company worth about $4.07tn. Volume on the two days averaged 445m shares, 2.6 times the average of the five sessions before, according to Yahoo Finance's daily history.

The selling was not spread across the chip sector. Over the same two days AMD rose 14.7 per cent and Broadcom 13.0 per cent, on closing prices from Yahoo Finance. Applied to the share counts on their latest 10-Q covers, from SEC EDGAR, those moves added about $108.5bn to AMD and $182.8bn to Broadcom: $291.3bn together, or 68 per cent of what Nvidia lost. CNBC put Broadcom's Monday gain at just over 10 per cent; on closing prices we make it 8.8 per cent. Share prices move for many reasons at once, and the arithmetic shows coincidence, not a transfer from one to the others.

The two days were not alike. On Monday, AMD and Broadcom together added about $221bn, more than the $192bn Nvidia lost, by our arithmetic. On Tuesday the pattern broke: Nvidia lost $236bn while the other two added about $71bn between them. Read that way, Monday looked like investors switching between chipmakers, and Tuesday like investors selling Nvidia.

The trigger was a weekend email. In a message to staff posted on Sunday 8 November, Huang wrote: "I was unfaithful to my wife." He also wrote, "I have fallen short of that responsibility," and said he would "cooperate fully" with the board's investigation. CNBC has reported that the affair was with Alice Huang, an executive at Megaspeed, a Singapore-based Nvidia customer. On 5 November three House committee chairmen asked Huang to brief Congress on his link to what they called a Chinese intelligence asset. The board's statement the same day said a committee of independent directors, "assisted by outside independent counsel", would run the inquiry, which we reported on Monday.

What does the lawsuit claim?

That Nvidia and Huang misled investors about Huang's relationship with Alice Huang and about Nvidia's dealings with Megaspeed. The suit was filed on Monday in the federal court in San Jose by the Rosen Law Firm for a shareholder named Mark Kistenmacher, Gizmodo reported. Rosen's notice says the defendants' statements were "materially false and/or misleading at all relevant times" and that investors were damaged when the true details entered the market. At least four other firms have announced investigations, according to Gizmodo.

The dates were chosen with care. The class period opens on Thursday 9 October 2025, the day before Singapore's police said publicly that they were investigating Megaspeed, the first entry in the paper trail we compiled on Tuesday. It closes on Sunday 8 November 2026, the day of Huang's email, so Monday's fall is the price drop the complaint relies on: the $7.97 a share that Gizmodo and others valued at about $192bn to $193bn. The lead-plaintiff deadline of 8 January 2027 is 60 days after the first notice, the window the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act allows.

The law also caps what such a suit can recover. Under the Private Securities Litigation Reform Act, damages per share are limited to the difference between what an investor paid and the average closing price over the 90 days after the corrective information reached the market. That average does not exist yet. Over the first two days it is $173.78, by our arithmetic, and it will move with every close until early February. For any buyer in the class period, the cap will be set by how the stock trades through Nvidia's results and the board's review, not by Monday's fall alone.

A securities complaint is the start of a long process, and many are dismissed. The Rosen suit has to show not only that statements were false but that they were material and made with the required state of mind. Nvidia had not responded publicly to the suit in the reports we read.

What are the analysts and officials saying?

President Donald Trump said the FBI was looking into the matter and added, "I think that he'll be in good shape," CNBC reported. Dan Ives of Yorkville Ives & Co. put the chance of Huang being removed as chief executive at roughly 15 per cent, and said a board review he expects to take four to six weeks would "create a clear overhang on the stock".

There is a market reason the rotation went to AMD and Broadcom in particular: both sell the processors and custom accelerators that are the nearest substitutes for Nvidia's in AI data centres. There is also an awkward symmetry. Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom are all among the seven suppliers that have agreed to buy $26bn of stock in Anthropic's listing, which is expected to price on 18 November.

Why it matters: the questions now facing Nvidia are about governance and export compliance rather than demand for its chips, and the market has priced them at roughly a tenth of the company in two sessions. Results can answer the demand question; they cannot answer the others, which sit with the board's committee, the FBI and Congress.

The calendar is short. The three House committee chairmen who wrote to Huang on 5 November asked for a reply by today, 11 November, and a closed briefing on 18 November. Nvidia reports its third-quarter results on Tuesday 17 November, with a call at 2 p.m. Pacific, seven days after Tuesday's close.
