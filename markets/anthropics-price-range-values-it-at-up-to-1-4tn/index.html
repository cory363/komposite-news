Anthropic's price range values it at up to $1.4tn

Anthropic will sell 825 million shares at $70 to $78 each, its amended filing and notice show, and seven of its chip and cloud suppliers will buy $26bn more privately at the same price. By our arithmetic the deal could top SpaceX's $85.7bn listing, depending on what is counted.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
November 10, 20266 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Anthropic plans to sell 825 million Class A shares at between $70 and $78 each in its initial public offering, which would raise up to $64.35bn before fees, the developer of the Claude AI models said on Tuesday. Of those shares, 801,351,354 are new stock from Anthropic and 23,648,646 come from existing holders, according to the company's notice launching its roadshow. The shares are to list on the Nasdaq Global Select Market under the ticker ANT.

The terms arrived in a second amendment to its registration statement, filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission at 06:29 Eastern time, 15 days after the first public filing. The fee table attached to it registers 948.75 million shares at a maximum of $78, a total of $74.0bn, and explains the difference: an extra 123.75 million shares that underwriters may buy, an option equal to 15 per cent of the deal. The draft underwriting agreement names Goldman Sachs and Morgan Stanley as representatives of the banks.

There is a second block of stock outside the offering. Seven companies, AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, Nvidia and TSMC, have agreed to buy $26bn of a separate Class N common stock at the IPO price in a concurrent private placement, CNBC reported from the filing. Those shares are not registered in the offering and are not covered by the underwriters' lock-up, CNBC said. The draft underwriting agreement carves out "a concurrent private placement" from the restrictions on Anthropic issuing new stock. At the top of the range, $26bn buys about 333 million shares; at the bottom, about 371 million.

What does the range say Anthropic is worth?

Between about $1.26tn and $1.41tn on a fully diluted basis. CNBC put the top-of-range figure at $1.405tn. Dividing that by $78 gives about 18.01 billion shares, by our arithmetic, and applying the same count gives $1.261tn at $70 and $1.333tn at the $74 midpoint. Against the $965bn valuation of Anthropic's Series H round in May, as reported by CNBC, the range is 31 to 46 per cent higher. Anthropic had been aiming for a $2tn valuation, CNBC reported, and some shareholders now regard even $1.5tn as in doubt.

The offering itself is small relative to the company. The 825 million shares come to about 4.6 per cent of the fully diluted count, or 5.3 per cent if the banks take their option, by our division. Measured against the run-rate revenue of about $81bn that the amended filing gives for the end of October, according to CNBC, the range implies a multiple of between 15.6 and 17.3 times, on our numbers. Run-rate revenue is the company's annualised figure for a single month, not reported annual revenue.

Is it bigger than SpaceX's listing?

It depends on what you count. SpaceX raised $85.7bn when it listed about five months ago, as CNBC reported. The 825 million shares alone would raise $57.75bn to $64.35bn, below that. Adding the supplier placement takes the total to $83.75bn at the bottom of the range and $90.35bn at the top, either side of SpaceX. If the underwriters also exercise their option at $78, Anthropic would raise $100.0bn in all, by our arithmetic. Of the $90.35bn top-of-range figure without the option, the placement would be 28.8 per cent.

What does the placement mean?

The seven buyers all sit in the supply chain for AI computing. Google and Microsoft sell cloud capacity, Nvidia, AMD and Broadcom design processors, TSMC manufactures them and Micron makes memory chips. They will pay the same price as public investors and will hold a different class of stock, Class N. The filing's terms for that class were not in the portion of the documents we could read, and Komposite makes no claim about its voting rights. It does mean that a large slice of the money raised will come from companies that sell computing to AI developers, the kind of circular arrangement Komposite noted when Nvidia considered guaranteeing loans for its own chips.

The prospectus pitches the listing as broad ownership. "Everyone who owns our stock, whether an institution or an individual, becomes part of that effort," the co-founders write, according to CNBC. People familiar with the deal told CNBC that Anthropic is aiming for about 20 per cent of shares to go to retail investors, against a usual 5 to 10 per cent, and that the order book was already covered across the range.

What do the accounts show now?

Faster growth and a larger accounting loss than in October. For the nine months to 30 September, revenue was $33.24bn, up roughly 1,200 per cent, and the net loss was $104bn, against $25.1bn a year earlier, CNBC reported from the filing. As in the first-half accounts, most of the loss comes from changes in the fair value of convertible notes and derivative liabilities, which rise with the company's own valuation. When Anthropic first filed, it reported first-half revenue of $16.3bn and a net loss of $80.7bn, of which $72.4bn came from that revaluation. The new figures imply third-quarter revenue of roughly $17bn, close to the whole of the first half, though we have not been able to check the quarterly split against the prospectus itself.

The lock-up runs on a familiar formula. Under the draft underwriting agreement, Anthropic may not issue more stock until the earlier of the second full trading day after it reports results for the quarter to 31 March 2027, or 180 days after the prospectus, with exceptions including the placement. Selling holders sign separate lock-up agreements.

Why it matters: this is the first chance for public investors to price a frontier AI developer directly, rather than through the chipmakers and cloud companies that supply it. The range is below the $2tn the company was reported to be seeking, above its last private price, and dependent on a $26bn commitment from its own suppliers. OpenAI, which cut the size and price of its latest round last month, wanted to close it before Anthropic listed. The structure of Anthropic's governance, including its plan to commit 5 per cent of its shares to a public benefit fund, will now be priced as well as debated.

The order of the next week is fixed. Nvidia, one of the placement buyers, reports its third-quarter results on Tuesday 17 November, with a call at 2 p.m. Pacific. Anthropic is expected to price its shares the following day and begin trading on Thursday 19 November, people familiar with the plans told CNBC. The final price, and whether the range moves before then, will be set in an amendment that has not yet been filed.
