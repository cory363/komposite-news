OKX Will Pay You 10% to Keep Your Dollars in Its App

The crypto exchange's new OKX Money app converts 50-plus currencies into stablecoins, offers 10% APY with no lockup, and comes with a Mastercard. It is the latest salvo in the stablecoin land grab.

David Park · Fintech Correspondent
October 8, 202624 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

OKX launched a standalone consumer app on October 6 that wants to be your bank account, your currency exchange and your savings account at the same time, as long as you are willing to hold the money as stablecoins.

OKX Money converts funding from more than 50 supported currencies into dollar-backed stablecoins and bundles the balances with payments, transfers and Mastercard-linked virtual and physical cards. Eligible balances in USDG can earn up to 10 percent APY with no staking and no lockup, paid weekly while the money remains available to spend. Users can hold USDG, USDC or USDT and move between them without conversion fees. Crypto.news reported that card purchases carry zero foreign exchange fees, with cashback of up to 10 percent on eligible spending wherever Mastercard is accepted.

The target customer is not the crypto native. OKX says roughly 70 percent of the users it wants to reach have never used a crypto application, and the initial rollout covers parts of Latin America, Africa, South Asia and the Middle East, regions where dollar access is valuable, banking infrastructure is thin and foreign exchange fees routinely add 2 to 5 percent to international purchases. The pitch is simple: the dollar, without the bank.

The 10% question

A 10 percent yield on dollar balances, with no lockup, will get attention, and it should get scrutiny too. CoinStats noted the rate applies to eligible USDG balances, which means the economics depend on the details OKX has not fully spelled out: caps, eligibility windows and how long a promotional rate lasts. The history of crypto yield products argues for reading the fine print before moving life savings into an app that launched this week.

That said, the product lands at a moment when the stablecoin market is being contested more fiercely than ever. Open USD, a new stablecoin backed by a syndicate including Stripe, Visa, Mastercard and Coinbase, went live on September 30 and reached $668 million in supply within days, in direct competition with Circle's USDC and Tether's USDT. London-based Noah closed a $38 million seed round this week on 538 percent year-to-date revenue growth, selling stablecoin rails to enterprises moving cross-border payments off correspondent banking. Its backers include Endeit Capital, FJ Labs, LocalGlobe and Felix Capital.

OKX is playing a different game from all of them. Where Circle and Tether fight over issuance and Noah sells infrastructure, OKX Money goes straight to the consumer with a single promise: your money moves at internet speed, earns a real yield and spends anywhere. If the unit economics hold past the promotional period, traditional neobanks in its launch markets have a problem. If they do not, OKX will have bought a lot of users with a rate it cannot sustain.

The regulators are watching

Products like OKX Money are arriving faster than the rulebooks written for them. In the United States, the GENIUS Act framework and the Treasury's ongoing rulemakings are still defining what stablecoin issuers and distributors can promise consumers. In the launch markets OKX is targeting, oversight ranges from welcoming to nonexistent, which is both the opportunity and the risk. A savings product marketed to first-time crypto users, offering double-digit yields, will eventually attract the attention of every consumer protection agency in every jurisdiction where it operates.

OKX's answer, presumably, is that the product is transparent about what backs the yield and that users can exit at any time, the no-lockup feature doing quiet regulatory work. Whether that satisfies regulators is an open question. What is not in question is the direction of travel. Between OKX Money, Open USD's syndicate launch and Noah's enterprise raise, this week made it clear that stablecoins have moved from crypto infrastructure to mainstream financial product. The companies that win the next phase will be the ones that can offer bank-like convenience with crypto-like speed, without blowing up in the process.

What it means

The deeper story is that stablecoins are quietly becoming the backend of consumer finance in markets the traditional banking system underserves. A 10 percent APY is the customer acquisition cost. The strategic prize is the deposit base, the transaction flow and the card interchange, all running on rails that settle in seconds and cost fractions of a cent.

Regulators will have views, particularly as these products scale beyond crypto's usual early adopters and into mainstream savings. For now, though, the competition is doing what competition does: better rates, lower fees and a race to onboard the next hundred million users. Your move, banks.
