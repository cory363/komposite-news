Embedded Finance Finds Its Natural Habitat in Vertical Software

Payments, lending, and insurance inside industry-specific platforms are outperforming standalone fintech, and the platforms know their pricing power.

Elena Vasquez · Fintech Correspondent
August 5, 2026 · 2 min read

The most successful fintech companies of the current cycle do not look like fintech companies. They look like software for dentists, contractors, restaurants, and freight brokers, with payments, lending, and insurance woven so deeply into the workflow that the financial product has no separate existence.

The advantage is data and distribution in one place. A platform that runs a business's operations sees revenue before a lender would, prices risk better than a stranger could, and offers the product at the exact moment of need. Attach rates that standalone fintechs spend fortunes pursuing arrive almost passively.

The economics have flowed accordingly. Vertical software firms now report financial services contributing large and growing shares of revenue, often at better margins than subscriptions, and their pricing negotiations with banking and payments partners reflect a clear-eyed understanding of who owns the customer.

The regulatory perimeter is the live question, as software companies perform bank-like functions through partner institutions. Supervisors have sharpened expectations for those partnerships, and the platforms treating compliance as core competence, rather than the partner's problem, are the ones expanding fastest.
