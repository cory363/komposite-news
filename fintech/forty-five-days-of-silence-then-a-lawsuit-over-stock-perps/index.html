Forty-five days of silence, then a lawsuit over stock perps

The CFTC let perpetual futures on US stocks take effect by not objecting for 45 days. A group backed by Cboe and Citadel Securities sued the next day. Briefing closed on Tuesday. Our timeline, and what Cboe told the CFTC in 2025.

Elena Vasquez · House byline
November 12, 20265 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

"Ultimately, it is up to the exchange listing any perpetual derivatives product to determine whether a new contract is to be listed as a future or swap." Cboe Global Markets wrote that sentence to the Commodity Futures Trading Commission in a letter of 21 May 2025. Eighteen months later Cboe is a member of the trade group suing the CFTC for letting exchanges do roughly that with perpetual futures on individual US stocks.

The Capital Markets Alliance Association, whose members include Cboe and Citadel Securities, asked a federal court in Manhattan on 3 November 2026 to set aside the CFTC's approval of the contracts, which took effect that day. Briefing on its request for a preliminary injunction closed on Tuesday 10 November. The Securities and Exchange Commission filed a brief on Monday that disputed the challengers' account of the dispute.

A perpetual future is a futures contract with no expiry, or a very distant one, kept close to the price of the underlying asset by periodic funding payments between the two sides. The products grew up on offshore crypto exchanges. Komposite reported in September that Kalshi, Coinbase and Bitnomial had filed to offer them on single US stocks.

How did they get approved?

By lapse. The exchanges self-certified the contracts with the CFTC on 18 September, and when the agency's 45-day review ended on 2 November without objection they were deemed approved, according to a summary by the law firm Cleary Gottlieb. The next day Chairman Michael Selig said in a statement that the contracts were approved "pursuant to Commission Regulation 40.3" as security futures, the category Congress put under joint CFTC and SEC oversight in 2000. "The CFTC and SEC have turned the page on that era," he said, referring to an era of regulatory uncertainty.

Five exchanges registered with the SEC so they could list security futures, by our count of the Cleary summary: Coinbase Derivatives, KalshiEX, Bitnomial Exchange, Nadex and ICE Futures U.S. Three of them, Coinbase Derivatives, Kalshi and Bitnomial, applied for more than 100 single-stock contracts between them, Finance Magnates reported. Kalshi started trading contracts on about 50 US stocks and exchange-traded funds on 5 November. Cboe told the court it counted about $375m of notional volume in 24 hours on the second day, according to the same report.

What is the case about?

Whether a contract with no delivery date can be a future at all. The alliance says the products are security-based swaps, which only the SEC can approve, because a security future requires delivery of a security at a future date. Judge Valerie Caproni, who heard the emergency application, found the alliance "is likely to succeed on the merits of its claim that the CFTC lacks statutory authority", in the words of her order as quoted by Cleary. She wrote that the CFTC's actions "seem to represent a major disruption to the equities market", Finance Magnates reported. She still refused the restraining order on 3 November, because the harm described was possible rather than likely. The case has since moved to Judge Lorna Schofield.

The CFTC's reply leans on Cboe's own record. Its brief calls the alliance a "sham entity" created for the case, Ledger Insights reported, and points to Cboe's bitcoin and ether "continuous futures", which run for up to ten years with daily funding payments. The 2025 letter supports part of that argument. It defined a perpetual future as a contract that has no expiration date or a long one, at least five years and "typically understood to be 10 or more", with a mechanism that keeps its price converging on spot. It also said Cboe was writing about futures "on financial instruments, which could include equities, indexes and digital assets."

In fairness to Cboe, the letter answered a CFTC question about futures in general. It did not address the line between security futures and security-based swaps on which the alliance's case turns, and it predates the single-stock filings by more than a year. The alliance describes itself as a trade association of financial institutions, according to Cleary; the CFTC disputes that it is a genuine membership group.

The SEC's brief takes neither side on the central question. It disputed the alliance's portrayal "of a CFTC gone rogue, and an SEC gone missing", according to Finance Magnates, but did not say whether the contracts are futures or swaps, and noted that the exchanges' rules remain under its review. Ledger Insights has reported that the SEC has until 31 December to decide whether to overturn them. Komposite has not confirmed that date.

Why it matters: the same question is now in two courts. CME Group's challenge to the CFTC's approval of crypto perpetuals, in which the agency argued CME could not have it both ways, raises a related question about what a perpetual contract is. The next fixed date in Manhattan is 25 November, when the CFTC's answer to the complaint is due, 22 days after the suit was filed. Judge Schofield has yet to set a date for argument on the injunction.
