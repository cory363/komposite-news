Mobile Money

London's biggest listing in five years opens below its price

Airtel Money made its debut on the London Stock Exchange on Friday at a valuation of about £5.3bn. Shares touched £2.00, then eased under the £1.96 offer price. The valuation is below the $8bn to $9bn range that had been estimated.

Elena Vasquez · Fintech Correspondent
October 10, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Airtel Money, the mobile-payments arm of Airtel Africa, began trading on the London Stock Exchange on Friday, in what BigGo Finance, citing Bloomberg data, described as London's largest initial public offering since Wise listed in 2021. The shares opened in conditional dealing at £1.96, the offer price, touched about £2.00 and then eased to about £1.94, Rediff reported. Reuters headlined its report that Airtel Money "slips below IPO offer price" in a $7bn debut.

The listing is formally of Airtel Mobile Commerce NV, under the ticker AMC. The offer sold 270 million existing shares for about £529m, valuing the company at about £5.3bn, the Economic Times said. The arithmetic checks: 270 million shares at £1.96 is £529.2m. BigGo said the sellers included the Qatar Investment Authority and TPG, and that Airtel Africa and Mastercard could sell up to 27 million more shares through an over-allotment option, which would lift total proceeds to about £582m. Airtel Africa itself said it would not sell any of its existing shares in the offer, Yahoo Finance UK reported, and BigGo said it took part only in the over-allotment tranche. It intends to remain a long-term strategic shareholder.

Only people who were allocated shares can trade for now. Unconditional trading is scheduled to begin on Wednesday, 14 October, when the broader market can buy. Susannah Streeter of the Wealth Club said the real test comes then, according to BigGo: "We'll see whether more investors are prepared to back its growth story and if this long-awaited flotation can help restore confidence in London as a place for ambitious businesses in other sectors to list."

The price was lower than hoped. The implied value of about $7bn is below the $8bn to $9bn range previously estimated, Rediff said. BigGo reported that Airtel Money lowered its valuation expectations twice during marketing and priced at a single fixed level instead of a range. By our subtraction, the gap to the earlier estimate is between $1bn and $2bn. International Finance Corporation, the World Bank's private-sector arm, agreed to buy up to £67.2m of shares from existing holders at the offer price as a cornerstone investor, BigGo said.

The business is large. Airtel Money operates in 13 sub-Saharan African countries and has about 53 million monthly active users, according to BigGo. It processed $213bn of transactions in the 12 months to 30 June 2026, which BigGo equated to roughly one-tenth of the region's gross domestic product, using World Bank data. In its 2026 financial year it reported revenue of $1.35bn and profit of $373m, BigGo said. Customers use it to store and send money, pay bills, withdraw cash and take loans and savings products.

Sunil Mittal, the founder of Airtel and chairman of Bharti Enterprises, presented the listing as a verdict on the UK. "This listing is a vote of confidence in the UK as an attractive global destination for investment, with a stable business and policy environment attractive for long-term investors," he said, according to Rediff. Ian Ferrao, Airtel Money's chief executive, called it "a landmark moment" for the company.

The ownership chain is layered. Bharti Airtel holds 79.2 per cent of Airtel Africa, which owns 77.85 per cent of Airtel Money, so Bharti's indirect economic interest is about 61.6 per cent, Rediff said.

The debut arrived in a week when other issuers pulled back. Komposite reported on Thursday that Firmus closed its IPO book without a clear price, and the Australian data-centre company has since withdrawn its listing. In fintech, Revolut's chief executive said this week that a US listing was always the target, though not before 2028. Airtel Money chose London, and for now it trades about where it was priced.
