Guidewire pays $1.7bn for an underwriting start-up

Guidewire, which sells core software to property and casualty insurers, agreed on Tuesday to buy London-based hyperexponential for $1.7bn in cash plus up to $400m tied to revenue targets. It will borrow $1bn to help pay for it.

Elena Vasquez · House byline
October 28, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Guidewire, a US maker of software for property and casualty insurers, agreed on Tuesday to buy hyperexponential, a London developer of AI underwriting software, for $1.7bn in cash plus up to $400m if revenue targets are met, the company said.

According to the announcement, made at Guidewire's Connections conference in Las Vegas, the base price is subject to adjustments, and the contingent payment of up to $400m depends on specified recurring revenue targets. Guidewire will fund the deal with $1.0bn of new term loan financing, committed by Bank of America and Citibank, and cash on hand. It expects the deal to close in the second quarter of its 2027 fiscal year. Centerview Partners advised Guidewire and J.P. Morgan advised hyperexponential.

Hyperexponential, known as hx, sells software that commercial insurers use to build pricing models and make underwriting decisions. The company says its platform has supported underwriting across more than $75bn of annual commercial premium, and names Aviva, Allianz, Sompo, Beazley, Markel, HDI and Cincinnati Financial among its customers. It was already a Guidewire technology partner. TechCrunch reported that hx was founded in 2017, has raised nearly $90m, according to PitchBook, from investors including Andreessen Horowitz and Battery Ventures, and that nearly 600 insurers in 40 countries use its software.

Amrit Santhirasenan, hx's chief executive and co-founder, said in the release that the company was founded "on the conviction that underwriting judgment is the highest-leverage work insurers do". Mike Rosenbaum, Guidewire's chief executive, said: "We're delighted to welcome Amrit Santhirasenan and the team at hyperexponential to Guidewire." TechCrunch described the aim as putting AI agents to work alongside human underwriters on analysis and pricing decisions.

Guidewire did not disclose hx's revenue, but its guidance allows an estimate. It said the deal will add at least 650 basis points to its fiscal 2027 growth in annual recurring revenue (ARR), the yearly value of subscription contracts, on an inorganic basis, and at least 150 basis points a year to organic growth from fiscal 2028. Guidewire ended fiscal 2026 with ARR of $1,242m, or $1,237m at July 2026 exchange rates, its results release says. Applying 6.5 percentage points to that base implies about $80m of ARR from hx, by our arithmetic. On that estimate the base price is about 21 times hx's recurring revenue, and about 26 times if the full earnout is paid.

The estimate has limits: 650 basis points is a floor, the ARR may be counted on Guidewire's definition rather than hx's, and the timing of the close will affect how much lands in fiscal 2027. The deal also changes Guidewire's balance sheet. By our subtraction, the cash portion of the base price is about $700m, more than half of the $1,215.3m in cash and investments Guidewire held at the end of July. The company said the deal will not affect its first-quarter guidance and that it will update its full-year forecast on its December earnings call.

Why it matters: underwriting and pricing are where insurers make or lose money, and the deal puts a specialist pricing tool inside software that many carriers already use to run policies and claims. For hx's investors, TechCrunch noted, it is a large return; by our arithmetic the base price is about 19 times the capital the company has raised.

Komposite has reported on how claims processing and other back-office work became a proving ground for agentic software, and on the spread of parametric insurance, where payouts follow measured events rather than adjusted claims. Guidewire's offer puts a price on the underwriting end of the same shift.
