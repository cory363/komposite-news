The middle layer absorbs aI's organizational shock

As reporting, coordination, and status work automate, companies are redefining what management means rather than simply thinning it.

Grace Lindqvist · Startups & Venture Reporter
June 10, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Much of middle management is information logistics: collecting status, reconciling plans, moving decisions up and context down. That is precisely the work AI systems absorb first, and organizations are confronting what the management layer is for when the logistics run themselves.

The crude response is thinning spans and layers, and some of that is occurring. The more considered redesigns redefine the role around what did not automate: developing people, making judgment calls under ambiguity, and owning outcomes across boundaries. Companies report rewriting manager expectations for the first time in a generation.

The transition is harder on identity than on org charts. Managers who measured their value in meetings run and reports produced are being asked to measure it in decisions improved and people grown, a shift that training budgets acknowledge and quarterly pressure often undermines.
