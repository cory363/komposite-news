Supply chain software consolidates around control towers

Point solutions for visibility, planning, and logistics are merging into platforms, and shippers are trading best-of-breed for one throat to choke.

Tom Kessler · Enterprise Technology Reporter
June 24, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The supply chain software landscape, fragmented across visibility trackers, planning engines, and logistics platforms, is consolidating into integrated control towers. Shippers exhausted by integrating a dozen point solutions are anchoring on platforms and pressuring the remaining specialists to plug in or sell.

The consolidation logic is data gravity. Planning improves with visibility data, execution improves with planning data, and the vendor that holds all three compounds advantages the specialists cannot match alone. Acquirers have spent accordingly, and the roll-up phase is visibly underway.

RelatedEnterprise software pricing strains under the weight of AI ›

Customers describe the trade honestly: fewer vendors and cleaner data against concentration risk and negotiating leverage surrendered. After the disruption years, most are choosing consolidation, on the theory that resilience begins with being able to see the whole chain in one place.
