SoftBank writes off $3.7bn of $9.5bn paid for power gear

SoftBank Group lost ¥768bn in the six months to September after booking ¥597.2bn against US power equipment it no longer plans to buy. Our reading of its accounting deck: 31 per cent of the prepayments are gone, and the second quarter alone lost ¥1.1tn.

Tom Kessler · House byline
November 11, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Page 8 of SoftBank Group's accounting presentation for the half year to 30 September carries a line that was not in its accounts a year ago: a ¥597.2bn ($3.7bn) loss on power-generation equipment for AI data centres in the US. A subsidiary called Silver Bands 7 had paid $9.5bn since April 2025 to reserve generating equipment ahead of expected shortages. SoftBank has now decided it does not need all of it.

SoftBank Group lost ¥768.0bn in the six months to 30 September 2026, against a profit of ¥2,924.1bn a year earlier, its presentation of Tuesday 10 November shows. The equipment loss accounts for 78 per cent of that, by our division, and it is a write-off of 31 per cent of the money SB7 had paid out.

The presentation splits the loss in two. $2.9bn is non-refundable advance payments for equipment that is no longer in the revised procurement plan. A further $0.9bn relates to modifying and settling contracts, and most of that cash has yet to be paid, from the third quarter onward. Of the $9.5bn, $6.6bn has been reassigned to equipment for PORTS-Pike, a power-generation project, and others. SoftBank goes out of its way to separate the two: "The loss of $3.7B is completely unrelated to PORTS-Pike." It also holds rights to rebates of up to $2.1bn on future purchases, on conditions, but has booked nothing for them: "No asset recognized; purchase timing and amounts undetermined."

Why do two loss figures circulate?

Because SoftBank reports half-years and some summaries report quarters. The ¥768.0bn is the April-to-September figure. SoftBank's first-quarter presentation showed a profit of ¥347.3bn for April to June. Subtracting one from the other gives a loss of about ¥1,115bn, or ¥1.1tn, for July to September alone, by our arithmetic. The same subtraction gives a profit of ¥2,502bn for the second quarter of 2025, when SoftBank was booking large gains on OpenAI.

This time there was no OpenAI gain. SoftBank carries its OpenAI holding at fair value, and the presentation says no gain or loss was recorded in the half because the valuation was unchanged from March. At 30 September the stake had cost $54.6bn and was carried at $99.6bn, a cumulative gain of $45.0bn, or 1.82 times cost. In October SoftBank paid the third $10bn tranche of its follow-on commitment, bringing its cumulative investment to $64.6bn for a stake of about 13 per cent. OpenAI's own fundraising has been cut in size and price since SoftBank's March mark was set, to a reported $1.25tn, from the $1.4tn or more it was reported to be seeking in October.

The gains that did arrive came from elsewhere. Intel, in which SoftBank bought $2bn of shares in August 2025, produced a ¥1,062.8bn valuation gain, partly offset by a ¥321.9bn derivative loss on prepaid forward contracts SoftBank had written against those shares. ByteDance added ¥569.4bn inside the Vision Funds. Warrants that Energy Global, a SoftBank group company, granted to OpenAI as incentives on data-centre leases cost ¥203.8bn as Energy Global's own value rose.

What is it costing to borrow?

More than twice as much as a year ago. Interest expense at the group's parent and financing companies was ¥676.4bn in the half, an increase of ¥407.2bn, which the presentation puts down to more borrowing and bonds and to writing off unamortised fees when bridge loans were repaid early. The finance presentation shows net debt at the parent of ¥11.3tn at 30 September, up from ¥8.2tn in March, and a loan-to-value ratio of 15.9 per cent, against a policy ceiling of 25 per cent in normal times.

Net asset value, the figure SoftBank asks investors to judge it by, was ¥59.8tn ($380bn) at the end of September. That is ¥19.7tn more than in March, which is how some reports described it as a record, but ¥12.5tn, or 17 per cent, less than the ¥72.3tn of June. SoftBank attributes the fall since June mainly to a ¥9.8tn decline in the value of its holdings and ¥2.0tn of currency effects.

Why it matters: SoftBank is funding its AI build-out largely with debt, which rose ¥3.1tn at the parent in six months, and its accounts now show what happens when a pre-ordered power plan changes. Other developers have been locking up generating capacity the same way, from leases to equipment orders, as Anthropic did with its first Canadian lease. SoftBank says the $0.9bn of settlement cash will go out from the third quarter, which ends on 31 December, and its third-quarter results will show how much of the $2.1bn of rebates, if any, it can use.
