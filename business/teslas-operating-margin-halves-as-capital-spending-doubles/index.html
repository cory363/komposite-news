Twice the capex, less than half the operating profit

Tesla's third-quarter operating income fell 56 per cent to $718m on flat revenue of $28.05bn, while capital spending more than doubled to $4.73bn. Optimus production has started in Fremont, and the company now guides to about $20bn of capex this year.

Priya Raghavan · House byline
October 22, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Tesla's third-quarter operating income fell 56 per cent to $718m while its capital spending more than doubled to $4.73bn, according to its quarterly update filed with the Securities and Exchange Commission on Wednesday. Revenue was flat at $28.05bn, and net income attributable to shareholders fell to $787m from $1.37bn.

Revenue was $28,046m against $28,095m a year earlier, down 0.2 per cent by our arithmetic. Gross profit rose to $5,494m, a GAAP gross margin of about 19.6 per cent against 18.0 per cent, so the squeeze came lower down: operating expenses rose 39 per cent to $4,776m by our arithmetic. The operating margin was about 2.6 per cent against 5.8 per cent, a fall of 322 basis points by the company's count. Non-GAAP net income was $1,697m.

Capital expenditure was $4,732m against $2,248m, or 2.1 times by our arithmetic, though below the $5,789m of the second quarter. Operating cash flow was $6,263m and free cash flow $1,531m, and cash and short-term investments rose to $45,447m. On the call, chief financial officer Vaibhav Taneja said Tesla was "revising our CapEx guidance to be approximately $20 billion for the current year", to be funded "primarily from our cash flows from operations", and that it had "secured capacity to borrow up to $30 billion" with no plans to draw on it this year, according to a transcript of the call.

The update explains the revenue mix. Energy generation and storage revenue fell to $2,881m from $3,415m, "partially due to tariff-related customer refunds", Tesla said, and regulatory credit revenue fell to $113m from $417m. Services and other revenue rose to $4,605m from $3,475m, with what the company called record gross profit of $734m. Deliveries fell 2 per cent to 486,532 vehicles while production rose 4 per cent to 464,391.

"Optimus production began in Fremont on our high-volume lines," Tesla said, describing the units as "the first production-quality bots that we have manufactured." On the call, Elon Musk, the chief executive, called it "what I'd call early production" and said the early part of the ramp "has a shallow slope and is somewhat bumpy because it is such a radically new product." He said it would take "at least a year, maybe a little longer" to reach a rate of 20,000 units a week. TechCrunch reported the target as 20,000 a week by the end of 2027. It is a goal, not an order book.

On autonomy, Tesla said it began giving Robotaxi rides in Cybercabs in Austin on 3 September, and that the service reached nearly two million cumulative unsupervised miles by the end of the quarter. Active Full Self-Driving (Supervised) customers, a measure that counts upfront purchases and subscriptions for software that still needs a watching driver, rose to 1.74m from 1.04m. Ashok Elluswamy, Tesla's vice president of AI software, said on the call: "We suspect somewhere around Q2 next year is when we can turn on some of the first customer vehicles to be part of the Robotaxi fleet."

Why it matters: Tesla is spending about twice as much on capital projects while earning less than half as much from operations, and its case now rests on robots, robotaxis and compute rather than on car volumes. Tesla says it will not draw on its new $30bn of borrowing capacity this year. The company's numbers are its own. Komposite reported on Wednesday on the special committees weighing a SpaceX and Tesla merger, which Mr Musk cannot attend.
