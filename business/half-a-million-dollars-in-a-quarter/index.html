Half a million dollars in a quarter

Blaize, the AI chip company that listed through a SPAC last year, says it booked about $0.5m of revenue in the third quarter and cut its outlook again. The full-year number now depends on a customer that has paid $0.2m of a $13.3m bill due in September, and on inventory the company needs cash to buy.

Jonathan Bright · Policy Editor
October 6, 20265 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Blaize Holdings, a California designer of AI inference chips that went public through a merger with the blank-cheque company BurTech Acquisition in January 2025, told investors late on Monday that it expects to report revenue of about $0.5m for the three months to September. A Form 8-K filed that evening says all of it was earned in August, from hardware sold to one customer in Europe. Blaize cut its full-year revenue guidance to between $32.0m and $36.0m. In mid-August it had said $40.0m to $43.0m.

Blaize shares closed on Monday at 43.6 cents. By early afternoon in New York on Tuesday they were changing hands at about 29 cents, a third lower, on Nasdaq data. In May the company sold 18.9 million new shares to the public at $1.85 each; on our arithmetic the stock is now about 84 per cent below that price. Rosenblatt's Kevin Cassidy downgraded the stock to neutral with a 40-cent target, and Craig-Hallum's Richard Shannon moved to hold, according to an Investing.com report carried by Yahoo Finance, with Cassidy pointing to limited cash, tight inventory and longer supplier lead times.

Blaize booked $14.7m of revenue in the first half, according to its second-quarter release. Add the third quarter's $0.5m and, on our calculation, the guidance requires $16.8m to $20.8m of revenue in the final three months of the year. That is more than it recognised in the first nine months combined.

The 8-K says where that revenue is supposed to come from. "The revised guidance is based on binding, non-cancellable purchase orders from NeoTensr and a new purchase order from an existing customer in process of signature." The NeoTensr orders sit under a contract signed in April for up to $50.0m, which was amended on 7 August into a take-or-pay arrangement with deliveries of servers intended to be completed by July 2027, according to the company's quarterly report. NeoTensr has issued one purchase order under it, for $13.7m, and paid a 3 per cent deposit.

The balance of that order, $13.3m, was due on 24 September. "On September 30, 2026, NeoTensr paid $0.2 million toward this balance, and the Company has agreed to extend the terms of this receivable, which is now expected to be paid during the fourth quarter of 2026," the filing says. "The Company has sufficient inventory on hand to partially fulfill the orders in the fourth quarter and is working with suppliers to obtain the balance before the year end." And: "Should there be delays in timing or in receiving payments from customers scheduled during the fourth quarter, the Company's revenue recognition for the year could be adversely affected."

That is a circular problem. "The revision reflects the Company's updated expectations regarding shipment timing and management of working capital to meet inventory purchase needs," the filing says. To recognise the revenue Blaize has to ship servers, and to ship them it has to buy parts. To buy parts it needs cash, and the $13.1m NeoTensr still owes, on our arithmetic, is exactly the kind of cash that would pay for them. At the end of June Blaize had $36.8m in cash, about $12.4m of it in Chinese banks and subject to currency controls, and it had used $41.3m in operations in the first half. "Given the Company's liquidity condition, as described above, the Company will be unable to continue to operate as a going concern and continue its operations unless it raises additional financing, if such financing is available at all," the quarterly report says. It has a committed equity facility with about $15.5m of capacity left, but cannot draw on it while the shares trade below $1.

This has happened before. In 2025 Blaize signed a strategic agreement with Starshine Computing Power Technology, a Hong Kong company, under which Starshine agreed to deliver at least $120.0m of revenue over 18 months, subject to purchase orders. Starshine issued one order, for $10.4m, and paid $1.6m. The remaining $8.8m has been handed to a collections agent that would keep 10 per cent of anything it recovers, and Blaize says it does not expect further orders. NeoTensr's first order, $23.8m in the fourth quarter of 2025, was paid in full this year. The quarterly report's receivables table lists a balance of the same size at the end of 2025 from a customer located in China; we infer it is NeoTensr's, though the table does not use names.

The company's filings add the other pressures. In July and August the Justice Department sent subpoenas and inquiries to Blaize and some of its employees and directors, seeking information about its business and its agreements with certain counterparties, and the SEC has made voluntary requests for similar material. Blaize says it is cooperating and cannot predict the outcome. Two proposed shareholder class actions, filed on 4 August and 17 September, allege that the company and some of its officers made false and misleading statements or omissions about the Starshine and NeoTensr contracts; the second also names the underwriters of the May offering. Blaize says the cases are at a preliminary stage. On 24 September Blaize began cutting about 26 per cent of its staff, a move it expects to save $7.5m to $8.4m a year, and on 28 September Nasdaq told it the closing bid price had been below $1 for 30 consecutive business days, giving it until 29 March 2027 to recover.

In August, chief financial officer Harminder Sehmi said: "Our revised full-year revenue outlook is weighted toward revenue from our largest customer and is based on binding, non-cancellable purchase orders that we believe we can fulfill with inventory commitments already made or planned." Chief executive Dinakar Munagala said then: "What changed is the pace at which opportunity converts into orders, alongside materially higher memory pricing." Monday's filing uses almost the same basis for a lower number, and the largest customer has since missed its payment date. At about 29 cents, and the 144.8 million shares the company had outstanding in mid-August, the market is valuing Blaize at about $42m on our arithmetic. That is not much more than the revenue the company still says it will book this year.
