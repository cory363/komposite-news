Offshore perps, an onshore operator

Intercontinental Exchange, owner of the New York Stock Exchange, has agreed to run US-regulated versions of Trade[XYZ]'s Hyperliquid-based perpetual futures and is taking a minority stake. The markets need regulatory approval, and no launch date has been set.

Marcus Oyelaran · House byline
October 31, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Intercontinental Exchange, the owner of the New York Stock Exchange, has signed a definitive agreement to operate US-regulated versions of Trade[XYZ]'s perpetual futures markets, which run on the Hyperliquid blockchain, and is taking a minority stake in the company, ICE said on Thursday. The plan needs regulatory approval.

A perpetual future is a futures contract with no expiry date. Instead of settling on a fixed day, it is held close to the price of the underlying asset by periodic payments between buyers and sellers. Trade[XYZ] uses Hyperliquid to offer eligible non-US users round-the-clock contracts on shares, equity indices, commodities and currencies. ICE will be the exclusive operator of the regulated versions and plans a new market and clearing structure dedicated to retail traders, with identity and anti-money-laundering checks, and will seek approval to support leveraged trading, the release says. CoinDesk reported that the venue, called ICE[XYZ], would also list crypto contracts.

ICE is careful to keep the new business apart from its old one. The retail market would run separately from its institutional futures and clearing operations, and the company says its commercial hedgers and institutional customers would have no exposure to retail trading or counterparty risk. Customers would reach the markets through futures commission merchants that become members, and through partners including ICE's joint venture with OKX, which itself is awaiting approval as a US broker-dealer and futures commission merchant. ICE says its existing business is more than 99 per cent institutional.

"We see these perp markets as additive to our futures markets, not a replacement for them," Jeffrey Sprecher, ICE's chair and chief executive, said in the release. He also pointed to a clear rise in retail participation, often around the clock, The Block reported. Trade[XYZ] described the planned market as "a new venue for price discovery, open whenever the world moves." Neither company disclosed the size of the stake. ICE said it was not expected to have a material effect on its 2026 adjusted results, capital return or plans to reduce debt.

Trade[XYZ] says its markets on Hyperliquid have handled more than $650bn of trading in just over a year, CoinDesk reported. By our arithmetic, taking "just over a year" as 13 months, that is no more than about $50bn a month on average. Open interest across HIP-3 markets, the Hyperliquid framework that lets outside builders such as Trade[XYZ] deploy contracts, reached $2.38bn in April, according to The Block. Hyperliquid's HYPE token rose about 15 per cent on Thursday to around $91, The Block said; CoinDesk later put the day's gain at nearly 20 per cent.

The deal also brought a new name to the venture. Stanley Druckenmiller, the investor, used his first post on X to say he had invested in Trade[XYZ], without giving an amount, CoinDesk reported. He wrote that the partnership would bring "24/7 perpetual futures on Hyperliquid to the United States", according to The Block. Jeff Yan, a Hyperliquid co-founder, wrote that he "could never have predicted the progress that we've made as a community in a single year to make this come true."

The regulatory path is open but contested. The Commodity Futures Trading Commission issued a staff advisory and no-action relief for US perpetual futures in May, The Block reported. CME Group sued the CFTC in June, arguing that perpetual contracts were wrongly classified as futures rather than swaps, and the regulator has asked the court to dismiss the case, CoinDesk reported. Neither ICE nor Trade[XYZ] has said when the markets could open or who will be eligible.

Why it matters: the two largest US futures exchange groups have now taken opposite positions on the same product. CME is in court arguing that the CFTC should not have approved a perpetual, as Komposite reported when the regulator answered CME's challenge. ICE, which has also invested in OKX and in Polymarket, is building a retail venue on a crypto chain's rails. The CFTC's approach to event contracts has already brought sports leagues to prediction markets; perpetuals on shares and commodities would extend that reach to round-the-clock leveraged trading.
