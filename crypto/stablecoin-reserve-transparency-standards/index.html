Stablecoin Reserve Reporting Converges on Common Standards

Monthly attestations are giving way to standardized disclosures that let treasurers compare issuers the way they compare money market funds.

Elena Vasquez · Fintech Correspondent
March 6, 2026 · 2 min read

Stablecoin issuers are learning to speak the language of money market funds. Reserve disclosures, once a patchwork of voluntary attestations in incompatible formats, are converging on standardized reporting of composition, maturity, and counterparties, driven by regulation in some jurisdictions and competitive pressure everywhere else.

The audience is corporate. Treasurers evaluating stablecoins for payments want the same visibility they get from a fund fact sheet, and issuers courting that demand have discovered that transparency is a product feature. Disclosure quality now appears in payment providers' issuer scorecards alongside redemption terms.

Standardization enables comparison, and comparison disciplines behavior. Analysts publishing side-by-side reserve breakdowns note measurable shifts toward shorter maturities and simpler holdings since standard formats took hold, the market rewarding conservatism it can finally see.

Gaps remain, particularly in real-time reporting and the treatment of reserves held across borders. But the direction is set, and it points toward stablecoins being evaluated with the tools of cash management rather than the vocabulary of crypto.
