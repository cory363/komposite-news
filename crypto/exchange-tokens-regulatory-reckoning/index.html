Exchange Tokens Face Their Regulatory Reckoning

Instruments that blur loyalty program, equity proxy, and fee discount are being forced into defined categories, with restructurings to match.

Jonathan Bright · Policy Editor
July 8, 2026 · 2 min read

The exchange token was crypto's most successful ambiguity: part loyalty program, part fee discount, part shadow equity in the platform that issued it. Regulatory frameworks now in force are ending the ambiguity, requiring issuers to define what the instruments are, and restructure them where the honest answer was uncomfortable.

The restructurings vary by jurisdiction and candor. Some platforms have converted token benefits into contractual rebate programs; others have wound tokens down entirely rather than defend their classification. Legal advisers describe the exercise as overdue hygiene that earlier market conditions let everyone defer.

Holders have absorbed the transitions with less drama than predicted, partly because the surviving programs are clearer about what they promise. The episode is a small case study in what regulation actually does to crypto structures: rarely bans them, frequently forces them to say what they are.
