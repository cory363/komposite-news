New market structure rules push crypto exchanges toward familiar shapes

Separation of exchange, brokerage, and custody functions is forcing reorganizations that make digital asset venues look more like their traditional counterparts.

Jonathan Bright · Policy Editor
August 10, 20262 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The vertically integrated crypto exchange, one company matching trades, holding customer assets, and dealing on its own account, is being regulated out of existence in major markets. Rules taking effect across several jurisdictions require the separation of functions that traditional finance divided long ago, and venues are reorganizing to comply.

The restructurings follow a common pattern: exchange operations in one entity, custody in another with fiduciary obligations, and any dealing activity walled off from both. Executives describe the process as expensive and clarifying in equal measure, since each entity now faces a defined rulebook rather than an ambiguous perimeter.

Consolidation is the predictable consequence. Compliance at this standard carries fixed costs that smaller venues struggle to absorb, and acquisitions of licensed but subscale platforms have accelerated. Analysts expect the exchange landscape to end up resembling equities: a few large venues, specialized niches, and thin margins on matching itself.

For institutional participants, the separation is the point. Allocators cite commingled functions as the primary lesson of past failures, and venues that restructured early report that the license wall has become a sales asset.
