Two coins, one exchange, no margin

Thailand has finalised rules for bitcoin and ether ETFs on its stock exchange, starting on 16 October. The design keeps Thai savers at home, keeps leverage out and gives local funds a domestic product to buy, as the number of active crypto traders falls.

Marcus Oyelaran · Blockchain & Digital Assets Editor
October 9, 20263 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Thailand's Securities and Exchange Commission on Thursday finalised rules allowing crypto exchange-traded funds to list on the country's main stock exchange, limited at first to bitcoin and ether, Cointelegraph reported. The rules take effect on Friday, 16 October, the regulator said in a statement. It issued 11 related notifications to build the framework, and said it would prescribe which crypto assets the funds may hold; it kept the discretion to widen the list later, Bitcoin.com News reported.

The design is narrow on purpose. Crypto ETFs must be listed only on the Stock Exchange of Thailand. Products linked to foreign crypto ETFs, such as depositary receipts, will not be permitted at first, and Thai brokers remain barred from helping retail investors buy overseas crypto ETFs, an exemption reserved for institutions and ultra-high-net-worth individuals, according to Cointelegraph. In practice, Bitcoin.com News wrote, a retail investor in Bangkok who wants a regulated bitcoin fund will be steered towards a Thai-listed product instead of a US one.

The products themselves are plain. A fund must be passive, seeking to track the price of the asset it holds, and must keep net exposure to a single cryptocurrency averaging at least 80 per cent of net asset value over each accounting year. The coins must be held with digital asset custodians regulated by the SEC. Brokers may not lend on margin to buy the funds, and investors must receive information about the products and confirm that they understand the risks before trading.

The rules also create domestic buyers. The SEC amended its rules so that mutual funds and private funds can invest in crypto ETFs established in Thailand; until now they could invest only in foreign ones, Cointelegraph reported. That could give the new products a base of institutional demand from the first day, Bitcoin.com News said.

The effective date is not a launch date. Each asset manager still needs SEC approval for its own product, so the first listings could come after 16 October, according to Bitcoin.com News. Issuers also have to complete fund registration, secure a listing and arrange custody, said Nirun Fuwattananukul, chief executive of Binance Thailand. "For many investors, particularly those who have been cautious about opening an exchange account or managing a wallet, an ETF through their existing brokerage account removes a real barrier," he told Cointelegraph. "Whether these products draw meaningful investment will depend on a few things: competitive fees, tight tracking of the underlying asset, and investor education."

The market the funds are entering has been shrinking. Active accounts on Thai crypto exchanges fell to 121,000 in July 2026, down 21.82 per cent in a single month and from roughly 265,000 in 2024, according to SEC data compiled in August and cited by Bitcoin.com News. By our arithmetic, that is less than half the 2024 level. Daily trading value fell 27.13 per cent from the month before, to 1,378 million baht, and stablecoins made up 66 per cent of trading volume, against 16 per cent for bitcoin.

The rules arrive after a long consultation. The SEC consulted on the principles in April and May and on draft regulations in August and September, and most respondents supported the proposals, the regulator said. It has also been working on the market's plumbing: in September it consulted on rules for market makers, liquidity providers and the exchanges used as price sources.

The timing is not kind. Bitcoin fell below $81,000 on 8 October, and US spot bitcoin ETFs had just recorded an outflow of $487m in one day, their heaviest daily exit since June, Bitcoin.com News reported. Thailand's bet is that a fund in an ordinary brokerage account, with no leverage and no foreign alternative for retail investors, will bring back some of the savers who have left the exchanges.
