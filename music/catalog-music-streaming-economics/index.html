Old songs are winning streaming, and it's not nostalgia alone

Catalog music dominates listening because the platforms, the playlists, and the economics all bend toward the proven.

Colin Abernathy · Culture Editor
July 20, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The defining statistic of the streaming era is that most listening goes to music that is not new. Catalog, the industry's word for everything past its release window, outdraws the new-release economy on the platforms, and the gap has reshaped what the music business spends money on.

The mechanics favor the familiar. Algorithms recommend what has already proven it retains listeners; a fifty-year-old song and a new single sit one tap apart, stripped of the friction that once made the past harder to reach than the present; and each sync placement or viral moment reintroduces a classic to an audience that streams it forever after. The investment world noticed, which is why catalogs now trade like the income-producing assets they are.

For legacy artists, the era is a second career that never requires a new record. For new ones, it is the hardest competitive landscape in the industry's history: every song ever recorded is on the same shelf, and the shelf never rotates the classics out.
