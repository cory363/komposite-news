OpenAI's agents go to the oilfield

ADNOC agreed a multi-year deal on Monday with OpenAI and G42 to run OpenAI's Frontier agent platform on its field planning and crude valuation. No price or timetable was given, and the system will run on G42's servers in the UAE.

Dana Whitfield · House byline
November 2, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Abu Dhabi National Oil Company has signed OpenAI and G42 to a multi-year partnership to run OpenAI's Frontier agent platform on its oilfield planning and crude valuation, ADNOC said on Monday. The agreement was signed at ADIPEC, the oil and gas conference in Abu Dhabi, and its value was not disclosed.

What does the release commit to? The three companies will build a custom "decision intelligence" capability that combines OpenAI's models with ADNOC's own industrial data, and it will run on hardware operated by G42 inside the UAE, according to the release. G42 is an Abu Dhabi AI group, and the release says it has worked with ADNOC for years through AIQ. ADNOC says human experts will keep oversight of consequential decisions. The release says no other energy company has yet deployed OpenAI Frontier. That is ADNOC's description, and it could not be independently verified.

Two uses come first. The first is field master planning, which ADNOC says will merge reservoir, facility and economic studies into a single decision, drawing on data from thousands of wells, several reservoirs layered on one another and hundreds of installations above ground. The second is what it calls barrel valuation: connected agents would forecast supply, demand, prices and margins across production, refining, logistics, sales and trading, so that each barrel is valued before it is produced. The release also mentions cyber defence support through an OpenAI initiative called Daybreak Blue, without detail.

The executives spoke in the release. "This is industrial AI at scale, pioneered here in Abu Dhabi," said Dr Sultan Ahmed Al Jaber, the UAE's Minister of Industry and Advanced Technology and ADNOC's managing director and group chief executive. "Together, we're shaping the future of AI-enabled energy," said Sam Altman, OpenAI's chief executive. Mansoor Al Mansoori, chief executive of G42 International, said: "You should not have to use AI to benefit from it." Al Jaber said the partnership would change ADNOC's field development, asset management and commercial operations, Gulf News reported. Sheikh Khaled bin Mohamed bin Zayed Al Nahyan, Abu Dhabi's crown prince, witnessed the signing.

What is Frontier? OpenAI launched it on 5 February as a platform for building, deploying and managing AI agents, including agents made by Google, Microsoft and Anthropic, CNBC reported at the time. It opened to a small set of customers first. The four that CNBC named were Uber, State Farm, Intuit and Thermo Fisher Scientific, none of them an energy company. OpenAI did not give pricing. Its chief financial officer, Sarah Friar, told CNBC then that business customers made up about 40 per cent of OpenAI's business and that she expected the share to approach 50 per cent by the end of the year.

By our arithmetic, ADNOC's signing comes 270 days after that launch. The company is not new to OpenAI's products: it says it has given more than 40,000 employees ChatGPT Enterprise, with adoption above 97 per cent, and counts more than 200 AI applications and 70 robotics uses either in service or being built. Those figures are ADNOC's own and were not independently verified. The new work also builds on ENERGYai, the subsurface AI platform ADNOC developed with AIQ.

What the release leaves out matters as much. It gives no contract value, no timetable beyond "multi-year", no measure of the savings it expects and no detail of how OpenAI's models will be hosted on G42's hardware. The claim that oilfield planning can be compressed by agents is a projection, and the release offers no test results.

Why it matters: Frontier's pitch is that agents can work across a company's systems rather than inside one tool. A national oil company is a large test of that idea, and running the system on G42's machines in the UAE keeps the data inside the country. OpenAI's own security posture has been under scrutiny: Komposite reported last month that it would train its frontier models without live internet access after a breach. The financing of the hardware behind such deals is its own story, as Nvidia's talks to guarantee Nscale's debt showed on Sunday.
