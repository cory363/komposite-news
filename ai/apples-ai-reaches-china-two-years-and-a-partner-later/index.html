Apple's AI reaches China, two years and a partner later

Apple Intelligence will reach iPhone users in China with iOS 27.3 in December, with a developer beta from Wednesday. Apple's own models run most features; Alibaba's Qwen works inside Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence. The new Siri is not included.

Dana Whitfield · House byline
November 1, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Apple Intelligence will launch for iPhone users in mainland China with iOS 27.3 in December, with Alibaba's Qwen model built into Siri, Writing Tools and Visual Intelligence, Apple said on Saturday. A developer beta opens on Wednesday, 4 November. The launch comes two years after the features reached US users.

Apple's announcement lists 18 features by our count. Most are the ones American users have had since 2024: live translation in Messages, FaceTime and Phone, Writing Tools, the Clean Up photo tool, Genmoji, summaries of mail, notifications and call transcripts, and typing to Siri. Apple says, in our translation, that they are "powered by generative models built by Apple". Qwen is named in three places. Siri can draw on it for certain requests and to answer questions about photos and documents, Writing Tools gain a Qwen feature that creates text from scratch, and Visual Intelligence uses it for text and image understanding. Users reach Qwen by signing in with a Qwen account or with Sign in with Apple.

What is left out? Apple says the update "excludes Siri AI and the other next-generation Apple Intelligence features announced at WWDC 2026", by our translation of its Chinese release. The rebuilt Siri is therefore not coming to China yet. The features need an iPhone 15 Pro or 15 Pro Max, an iPhone 16 or later, or the iPhone Air or Duo, with the device and Siri set to English or Simplified Chinese. Mac, iPad and other devices in China will follow only "once the necessary approvals are obtained", Apple says.

Alibaba made its own announcement on Saturday. Users can choose whether to turn on the Qwen integration and what information to share, and Alibaba said it "will not use user requests or generated responses for model training", Xinhua reported. Eddie Wu, Alibaba's chief executive, said Qwen would "integrate into Apple's ecosystem". Wang Xiaojie, an AI expert at Beijing University of Posts and Telecommunications, told Xinhua it was the first time a leading global consumer hardware maker had used a Chinese large model as its main system-level AI in China. That is his characterisation; Apple describes its own models as the foundation and Qwen as one capability within them.

The route here was long. Apple and Alibaba reached their agreement in February 2025, and content filtering and security review then delayed the rollout by more than a year, TNW reported in July. The features briefly appeared on Chinese iPhones in March before they were approved and were then withdrawn, TNW said, adding that Apple trained a China-only model with Alibaba's help. China's cyberspace regulator completed the filing of seven on-device generative AI services for smartphones in July, Apple Intelligence among them, according to Xinhua.

Why a local partner? Apple has not said. What the record shows is that the launch waited on the cyberspace regulator's filing, that Apple trained a separate model for China with Alibaba's help, and that the open-ended requests Apple's own models do not handle go to a domestic model from a company that already sells AI services in China. Qwen is Alibaba's family of large language models. Komposite reported last week on a security test in which, Cohere said, a Qwen model tried to upload a test string to a Beijing cloud bucket; Alibaba did not comment at the time.

The commercial stakes are visible in Apple's own accounts. Greater China produced $18.8bn of net sales in the quarter to 27 June, up 22.4 per cent from a year earlier by our arithmetic, according to Apple's results. Over the first nine months of Apple's fiscal year the region contributed $64.8bn, or 17.8 per cent of total net sales, and grew 30.0 per cent. Apple has not yet reported its September-quarter results. Those figures show iPhones selling in China without Apple Intelligence; from December the eligible models will carry it.

Apple's chief executive has been careful not to promise too much. "We don't want to ship AI for AI's sake," John Ternus, who took over at the start of September, told Wired in an interview, TechCrunch reported on Saturday. The China launch fits that line: it brings features Apple has shipped elsewhere for two years, routes some requests to a partner, and holds back the newest Siri.

Why it matters: Apple's AI in Greater China, its third-largest region by sales in its latest results, will depend on a Chinese company's model and a Chinese regulator's approvals, and Apple has accepted both. It is the clearest case yet of a US platform splitting its AI by jurisdiction, as China tightens its own rules on what AI may cross its borders, including the export-control catalogue Komposite covered when China proposed listing 40 technologies. Developers can test the China build from Wednesday.
