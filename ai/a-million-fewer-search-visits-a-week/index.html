A million fewer search visits a week

Reddit's revenue rose 53 per cent in the third quarter, but it said search referrals fell "meaningfully" as search becomes AI-powered, warned that daily users could fall by a low-double-digit percentage, and guided below forecasts. The shares fell about a quarter.

Dana Whitfield · House byline
October 30, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

Reddit said on Thursday that visits from search engines fell "meaningfully" in the third quarter as search becomes an AI-powered destination, and that its daily users could fall by a low-double-digit percentage this quarter, according to its letter to shareholders. Its shares fell about a quarter on Friday.

The quarter itself was strong. Revenue rose 53 per cent to $895m, advertising revenue 54 per cent to $847m and net income 74 per cent to $283m, or $1.40 a diluted share, the results release shows. Adjusted EBITDA was $402m, a margin of 44.9 per cent. Daily active uniques, the company's main user measure, rose 12 per cent to 130.3m, but fell 2 per cent in the United States to 50.7m. For the fourth quarter Reddit guided revenue of $1,000m to $1,030m and adjusted EBITDA of $480m to $510m.

The letter is unusually direct. "Our search referral traffic was down meaningfully in Q3, with the decline accelerating later in the quarter," it says. "During October, we have seen that search traffic has dropped by more than 1 million referrals on average per week." The biggest effect was on logged-out and web visitors, Reddit said, though some flowed through to logged-in and app users, since "search referral is a common source of reengagement for users". It added: "If this rate of decline continues, current trends would suggest that total DAUqs could fall in Q4, by a low-double-digit percentage from our current base of 130 million DAUqs."

The letter does not name Google. It describes the change in general terms: "Web search is evolving from a gateway to the web to becoming an AI-powered destination itself." Reddit's answer is to depend less on visitors who never sign in. "In Q4 we'll begin fully testing this in select markets outside the U.S.," the letter says of a logged-in-only Reddit, adding that "it is better for our business because we will have more signals for ads targeting". Steve Huffman, the founder and chief executive, said in the release that "the Reddit experience captures the human experience in a way that no other platform can replicate".

Investors looked past the quarter. Analysts had expected fourth-quarter revenue of $1.05bn, Investing.com reported, and the shares fell about 22 per cent shortly after Friday's open. KeyBanc cut the stock to Sector Weight, writing that "our thesis is not playing out; search traffic is a larger headwind than envisioned", and Citizens cut it to Market Perform. John Colantuoni of Jefferies wrote that "delivery of strong Revenue/EBITDA growth (53%/70%) was overshadowed by worsening U.S. DAU declines". The fall reached 25 per cent, Yahoo Finance reported, which noted the shares had closed on Thursday at about 15 times forward earnings against a one-year average of 26.

By our arithmetic, the guidance midpoint of $1,015m is 3.3 per cent below that consensus but still 13.4 per cent above the third quarter, at an implied adjusted EBITDA margin of about 49 per cent. Logged-out users, the group most exposed to search, are 59 per cent of daily users worldwide and 54 per cent in the United States. Reading "low-double-digit" as 10 to 13 per cent, the warning implies 113m to 117m daily users if the October trend holds. Revenue per US user, $13.82, is 5.7 times the international figure, which is why a 2 per cent fall at home weighs more than a 24 per cent rise abroad.

Why it matters: Reddit has now put a weekly number on the traffic it is losing as search changes, in a document filed with its regulator. Alphabet's search revenue rose 12 per cent to $63.4bn in the quarter, Yahoo Finance noted, even as Alphabet's own shares fell on spending that ran ahead of its cash. The visits that once followed a search result are increasingly kept inside the answer, which is the market a new class of start-ups is trying to sell into, as Komposite reported when Profound raised $180m to get brands named in AI answers.
