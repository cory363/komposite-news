The Practical Effects Comeback Is Really About Trust

Audiences reward films that build what they show, and productions are relearning crafts the digital era nearly retired.

Colin Abernathy · Culture Editor
June 29, 2026 · 2 min read

Somewhere in the last decade, 'shot practically' became a marketing claim. Productions now advertise their stunts, miniatures, and built sets the way they once advertised their rendering budgets, and audiences reward the promise reliably enough that studios keep making it.

The craft revival is real, not just rhetorical. Model shops, animatronics teams, and stunt units that spent the 2000s shrinking are training apprentices again, usually working in deliberate combination with digital tools rather than in opposition to them; the effect that reads as real is typically a physical foundation finished invisibly in post.

The deeper current is trust. In an era when any image can be synthesized, the knowledge that something happened in front of a camera has acquired its own value, on screen and in the marketing. Practicality has become a promise about honesty, which is a strange and telling burden for entertainment to carry.
