The disc endures: physical media's stubborn second life

As streaming libraries shuffle and shrink, collectors and boutique labels have turned owning films into a movement.

Colin Abernathy · Culture Editor
June 8, 2026 · 2 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

The disc was declared dead the moment streaming arrived, and the mourners keep having to reschedule. Physical media persists as a smaller but intensely committed market: boutique labels selling restored editions with essays and commentaries, collectors who treat shelves as libraries, and buyers who learned from vanishing streaming titles that access is not ownership.

The boutique economics work precisely because the audience is devoted rather than broad. Limited editions sell out on announcement; packaging and supplements justify prices that would have seemed absurd in the rental era; and the labels function as curators, deciding which overlooked films get the deluxe treatment and, with it, a second reputation.

The persistence carries an argument about culture's memory. Streaming catalogs are leases that expire quietly; a disc on a shelf answers to no licensing renewal. The collectors know exactly what they are doing: keeping a version of film history that cannot be removed from them.
