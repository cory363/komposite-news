A hundred trillion won, and short until 2030

SK hynix reported third-quarter revenue of ₩104.6tn on Wednesday, its first quarter above ₩100tn, with an operating margin of 80 per cent. It said demand for memory would exceed supply through 2030 and that 14 customers have signed long-term agreements.

Tom Kessler · House byline
October 28, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

SK hynix, the South Korean memory maker, reported record third-quarter revenue of ₩104.6tn on Wednesday, its first quarter above ₩100tn, and said demand for memory chips would exceed supply through 2030, according to its results release.

The release gives revenue of ₩104,615.5bn for the three months to September, up 32 per cent on the second quarter and 328 per cent on a year earlier. Operating profit was ₩83,333.8bn, up 38 per cent on the quarter and 632 per cent on the year, for an operating margin of 80 per cent, against 76 per cent in the second quarter and 47 per cent a year ago. Net income was ₩39,775.6bn, a net margin of 38 per cent. The figures are preliminary and prepared under Korean IFRS, the company said.

Net income fell 58 per cent from the second quarter, when it was ₩93,922.6bn, even as operating profit rose. By our subtraction, second-quarter net income exceeded operating profit by ₩33.4tn, while third-quarter net income was ₩43.6tn below it. The results release does not explain either gap; on the operating line, every measure rose.

The outlook is the part customers will read most closely. The company said it "expects the structure in which demand exceeds supply to persist through 2030", citing the technical difficulty of new products and the long lead times for new fabrication plants. It has signed long-term agreements (LTAs), contracts that commit volumes for several years, with 14 major customers including global cloud providers. The release says the volume secured so far corresponds to "more than 50% of total production capacity for 2027 and 2028"; the contracts typically run five years and use a price range that moves with market conditions, and some customers are discussing extensions.

On products, SK hynix said server DDR5 and the start of HBM4 shipments drove DRAM sales. HBM, or high-bandwidth memory, is stacked DRAM packaged next to AI accelerators. The company expects HBM4 to pass the previous generation, HBM3E, in both shipments and revenue in the fourth quarter, and said its M15X fab will reach full capacity by the end of the year, with the first phase of its Yongin cluster entering full operation next year. It gave no capital expenditure figure, saying only that infrastructure investment will rise significantly and that it will adjust its scale and timing flexibly.

The cash is going back to shareholders at a pace that matches the margins. The company has a 2026 target of about ₩100tn in total returns, including at least ₩30tn of dividends and at least ₩60tn of buybacks and cancellations. It declared a third-quarter dividend of ₩20,000 a share, about ₩14tn in total, half of it a special dividend sharing gains from its investment in Kioxia, and a ₩20tn buyback, of which about ₩10tn will fund employee compensation and is excluded from the returns total. Net cash stood at ₩98.7tn at the end of September: ₩114.5tn of cash against ₩15.8tn of borrowings. The company also said it had stopped reviewing an initial public offering of its Solidigm subsidiary. "The IPO was intended to raise external capital for the expansion of Solidigm's production facilities," said Kwak Noh-Jung, the chief executive.

What the release says and how it was reported part in one place. The Register reported operating profit of ₩87.68tn, up 486 per cent on a year earlier. The company's own table gives ₩83,333.8bn and 632 per cent, and its numbers are internally consistent: ₩11,383.4bn a year earlier multiplied by 7.32 gives the third-quarter figure, by our arithmetic. We use the company's figures. The Register also reported that Kim Woo-hyun, the chief financial officer, said PC and mobile shipment volumes "are expected to decrease", a remark not in the release, and that the shares rose about 3 per cent in after-hours trading.

For scale, Samsung Electronics guided on 8 October to about ₩107.4tn of third-quarter operating profit on about ₩195tn of sales across all of its businesses, a margin of about 55 per cent by our arithmetic. SK hynix, which makes only memory, earned nearly four-fifths as much operating profit on a little over half the sales.

Why it matters: the shortage statement is a supplier's forecast, and a supplier has reason to describe its market as tight while it negotiates five-year contracts. But it is backed by contracts rather than adjectives: more than half of two years of future capacity has been committed to 14 buyers before some of the plants that will produce it are finished. For the cloud companies building AI data centres, memory is being priced in advance and in ranges; for PC and phone makers outside those agreements, it means competing for what is left.

The figures extend a pattern Komposite has tracked across the memory industry. In September we reported that Micron's gross margin had reached 84.6 per cent on almost flat bit shipments, with customers paying deposits in advance. Korea's export-credit researchers have put the data centre AI chip market at $860bn by 2030, the same year SK hynix now gives for the end of the shortage.
