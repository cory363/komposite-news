Infineon now expects close to €4bn from AI power supplies

Infineon told investors in February that power chips for AI data centres would bring in about €2.5bn in its 2027 fiscal year. On Tuesday it said close to €4bn. Our compilation of its targets, and of the segment that now earns most of its profit growth.

Tom Kessler · House byline
November 12, 20264 min read
ShareXLinkedInBlueskyFacebookEmail

In February, Infineon Technologies said its chips for powering AI data centres would bring in about €2.5bn in the fiscal year to September 2027. In May it repeated the figure. On Tuesday 10 November the German company put it at close to €4bn, in its results for the 2026 fiscal year.

Infineon, the Munich-area maker of power semiconductors, expects revenue of about €20bn in its 2027 fiscal year, up more than 20 per cent, with sales of power-supply chips for data centres rising to close to €4bn. That target is 60 per cent above the €2.5bn it gave in February, by our arithmetic, and would be about a fifth of group revenue.

These are chips that convert and regulate electricity for the servers that run AI models. Infineon reports them inside a segment it called Power & Sensor Systems until a reorganisation on 1 July, after which it reports Automotive, Edge Systems and Power Systems. The segment that holds the data-centre business is where the year's profit growth came from.

How has the target moved?

Upward once, sharply, after nine months of standing still. The €1.5bn and €2.5bn figures first appeared in the February release, when Infineon also raised its investment plan for the year to about €2.7bn, from €2.2bn, to build capacity for these products. In August it did not update the revenue figures but disclosed that several AI customers had signed or were negotiating multi-year capacity reservation agreements worth "a high single-digit billion euro amount", with some prepayments. The new target arrived 279 days after the first, by our count.

The November release does not say how much data-centre revenue Infineon actually booked in the year just ended, and it does not update the value of the capacity agreements. If the 2026 figure landed near the €1.5bn guided in May, the step to close to €4bn would supply about two-thirds of the roughly €3.65bn of extra group revenue Infineon is forecasting, by our arithmetic. That is an inference from the company's own targets, not a figure it has published.

Where did the profit come from?

Almost entirely from power and sensors. Group revenue rose 12 per cent to €16.353bn in the year and the segment result, Infineon's main profit measure, rose €663m to €3.223bn. Power & Sensor Systems alone added €700m, to €1,374m, so the rest of the group earned slightly less than a year earlier, by our subtraction. The segment's margin rose in every quarter, from 17.4 per cent to 30.8 per cent.

The other segments were mixed. Automotive, still the largest by revenue at €7,672m, grew 4 per cent and its result rose 2 per cent. Green Industrial Power grew revenue 10 per cent but its result fell 9 per cent. Connected Secure Systems shrank on both counts.

"Infineon closed the fiscal year 2026 with another record quarter and enters the new fiscal year with continued strong momentum," Jochen Hanebeck, the chief executive, said. He listed data-centre power supplies first among the businesses expected to drive 2027, ahead of grid infrastructure, electric vehicles and software-defined vehicles.

The company plans to spend €3.0bn to €3.5bn in fiscal 2027, depending on demand, on its fourth manufacturing building in Dresden, silicon carbide capacity in Kulim, Malaysia, and the start of gallium nitride production on 300-millimetre wafers in Villach, Austria. The plan assumes a euro at $1.15. The power the chips manage is itself scarce: SoftBank this week wrote off $3.7bn of prepayments for generating equipment, and we have reported how power availability has become the constraint on building computing capacity.

The first test of the new target is the quarter to December, for which Infineon guides revenue of about €4.6bn and a segment margin of about 23 per cent, slightly below the fourth quarter's 23.8 per cent. It proposes a dividend of €0.38 a share, up €0.03, for approval at its annual meeting in February.
